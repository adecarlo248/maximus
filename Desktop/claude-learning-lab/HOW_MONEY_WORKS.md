## The 7 Principles of How Money Works™

### 1. TAKE CONTROL

Financial security comes from controlling the things **you can control**.

**You CANNOT control:**
- The future of Social Security / CPP
- Your employer
- Taxes
- Inflation
- Rising costs
- The risk of a single investment

**You CAN control:**
- Saving for retirement
- Other sources of income
- Ways to reduce your taxes
- Maximizing your savings
- Saving more
- Diversity of your investment choices

**Action Steps to Take Control:**
- **Pay Yourself First** — commit to saving before spending
- **Know Your Financial Independence Number (FIN)** — the amount of savings you need to stop working
- **Adjust Your Priorities** — "It's not what you make, it's what you keep"
- **Change Your Thinking** — your money mindset determines your financial outcome
- **Adjust Your Lifestyle** — distinguish *needs* vs. *wants*
- **Earn Additional Income** — a part-time income stream can accelerate savings
- **Realign Your Assets** — move money from low-interest savings to higher-return investments; replace expensive cash value life insurance with term
- **Avoid the Credit Trap** — pay your balance in full monthly; pay with cash to spend less
- **Set Goals and Have a Plan** — without a destination you can't reach it

**Goal Setting Tips:**
1. Make a list — describe goals in detail
2. Prioritize — which are most attainable or most important?
3. Get personal — the more personal, the more motivated you'll be
4. Do the math — figure out how much you need to save
5. Create a timeline — so you can stay on track

> "Retirement is an amount of money, not an age."

### 2. PAY YOURSELF FIRST

**Problem:** At the end of the month, most people have nothing left to save.
**Solution:** At the beginning of the month, before paying anyone else, set aside 10% of income.

> "Paying yourself first may be the single most important concept in this book."

**The Three Accounts You Need:**

| Account | Goal | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| **Emergency Account** | Min. 3 months income (0–2 year purchases) | Emergencies, unexpected medical, car repairs |
| **Short-Term Account** | Min. 6 months income (3–5 year purchases) | Job loss reserve, house down payment |
| **Wealth Building Account** | Enough to retire in dignity | Retirement income, medical, legacy, charitable giving |

> "Put yourself at the head of the line. Treat savings like any other recurring bill."

### 3. PAY OFF DEBT

> "Of all the threats to your financial security, none is more dangerous than debt."

**Revolving vs. Fixed Debt:**
- **Revolving debt** (credit cards): Interest compounds daily. No fixed payoff. Balance can grow forever.
- **Fixed debt**: Set payment schedule. Definite payoff date.

**Example of Credit Card Debt Impact:**
- $17,000 at 18% with minimum revolving payments = **17 years, 2 months** to pay off and **$12,500 in interest**
- $17,000 at 18% with $595/month fixed payment = **3 years, 2 months** to pay off and **$5,370 in interest**

**One-time purchase example:**
- $3,000 credit card purchase at 18% with minimum payments → takes 10+ years to pay off, costs $2,002 in extra interest (total: $5,002)

**Debt Stacking Strategy:**
1. Make consistent minimum payments on ALL debts
2. Identify the "target account" (strategic order by interest rate and balance)
3. When the first debt is paid off, roll that payment into the next target account
4. Continue until all debts are eliminated
5. Then redirect the full monthly payment toward wealth building

**Debt Stacking Example Result (total $2,567/month payment):**
- **Without Debt Stacking:** 25 years, 1 month to pay off / $205,279 in interest
- **With Debt Stacking:** 10 years, 6 months to pay off / $92,733 in interest
- **Savings:** 14.5 years sooner + $112,546 interest avoided

**5 Tips to Raise Your Credit Score:**
1. Know your score — monitor it regularly (FICO: 800+ Excellent / 740–799 Very Good / 670–739 Good / 580–669 Fair / below 580 Poor)
2. Don't skip a payment — late payments stay on your report for 7 years
3. Keep balances low on credit cards
4. Catch up overdue accounts
5. Don't apply for credit too often (too many inquiries hurt your score)

**Common Credit Mistakes to Avoid:**
- Not valuing your credit
- Maxing out your cards
- Not monitoring your credit score
- Not knowing your interest rate and fees
- Not being aware of your total debt

### 4. DISCOVER THE RULE OF 72

> "Albert Einstein called compound interest the most powerful force in the universe."

**The Rule of 72:** Divide 72 by the rate of return to find how many years it takes your money to double.

| Rate | Years to Double |
|---|---|
| 3% | 24 years |
| 6% | 12 years |
| 12% | 6 years |

**Compound Growth Example — $10,000 one-time investment:**
| Rate | After 48 Years |
|---|---|
| 3% | $40,000 |
| 6% | $160,000 |
| 12% | $2,560,000 |

**Power of Starting Early:**
- $1,000 invested at birth at 9% = **$339,737** at age 65
- $1,000 invested at age 16 at 9% = **$80,927** at age 65
- $1,000 invested at age 40 at 9% = **$9,408** at age 65

**Key: Compound Interest vs. Simple Interest**
- Simple interest on $1,000 at 9% for 65 years = $5,850
- Compound interest on $1,000 at 9% for 65 years = **$339,700**
- That's the power of reinvesting earnings year after year

**Just a Little More Has a Big Impact ($100/month vs. $20/month at 9%):**

| Years | $20/month | $100/month |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | $3,900 | $19,500 |
| 20 | $13,460 | $67,300 |
| 30 | $36,890 | $184,450 |
| 40 | $94,330 | $471,650 |

> "Just $100 a month compounding at 9% totals more than $470,000 after 40 years."

**The High Cost of Waiting (to save $1,000,000 by age 65):**

| Start Age | Monthly Savings Required |
|---|---|
| 25 | $213 |
| 35 | $543 (2.5x more) |
| 45 | $1,487 (7x more) |
| 55 | $5,130 (24x more) |

**Importance of Rate of Return — $1,000 invested at birth to age 65:**
- 3% = $7,000
- 6% = $48,900
- 9% = $339,700

> "You can't afford the high cost of waiting."

### 5. BUY THE RIGHT KIND OF LIFE INSURANCE

> "Life insurance should really be called 'income protection.'"

**Who needs life insurance:**
- Anyone with dependents (spouse, children, others relying on your income)
- Even a non-earning spouse may need coverage if child care costs would result from their death

**Who probably doesn't need it:**
- Singles with no dependents and significant cash resources

**The Theory of Decreasing Responsibility:**
- Early life: young children, high debt, mortgage → need a LOT of coverage
- Later life: grown kids, lower debt, mortgage paid → need much less
- At retirement: need income, not insurance — you should be "self-insured"

> "Retirement is a time when there really is no substitute for cash."

**Term vs. Cash Value Life Insurance:**

| | TERM | CASH VALUE (Whole / Universal Life) |
|---|---|---|
| **Premium** | Lower initial premium | Typically higher |
| **Purpose** | Pure death protection | Death benefit + bundled "investment" |
| **Investment Component** | None (you invest separately and control it) | Included but hidden/hard to compare |
| **Death Benefit** | High coverage for the cost | Lower coverage |
| **Cash Access** | N/A | You borrow your OWN money and pay interest |
| **At Death** | Full benefit paid | Traditional: you get EITHER benefit OR cash value, not both |

**How Much Life Insurance Do You Need?**
> 10x your annual salary is a good rule of thumb.

**Buy Term and Invest the Difference — Canadian Example:**
- $100,000 whole life policy: $106.87/month premium
- $300,000 term life policy (35-year): $52.64/month premium
- Difference = $54.23/month ($651/year) available to invest
- That $651/year invested at 9% over the term = $160,729 (vs. $36,523 cash value in whole life)
- **3x the death benefit, lower premium, AND a separate investment**

**Universal Life Insurance vs. Buy Term + TFSA:**
- UL $300,000 policy: $380/month, cash value at 65 = $24,792
- Term $300,000 + $313/month into TFSA at 9%: same $380 total spend, value at 65 = **$577,320**

**Primerica's Three "Nevers" of Life Insurance:**
1. **NEVER** buy cash value / whole life / universal life insurance
2. **NEVER** buy life insurance as an investment
3. **NEVER** buy a policy that pays dividends

**Warning Signs — Products to Avoid:**
- Cash Value Life Insurance
- Whole Life Insurance
- Permanent Life Insurance
- Universal Life Insurance
- Insured Retirement Plan (IRP)

> "Living Benefits are not exactly what you might think. Cash value grows 'tax deferred' — NOT 'tax free.'"

**Group Life Insurance Warning:**
- You don't OWN a group policy — if you leave employment, you may lose coverage
- Short-term or long-term disability can trigger loss of group coverage

**What Experts Say:**
- Suze Orman: "There is only one kind of life insurance that makes sense for the vast majority of us: term life insurance."
- Jane Bryant Quinn: "Term insurance is pure protection... You can buy large amounts of coverage for modest amounts of money — and big policies are what your spouse and children need."

### 6. DEFER TAXES

> "The harder you work to get ahead and build your income, the more taxes you pay."

**Key Concepts:**
- **Deductibility** — subtract from gross income *before* taxes are calculated (reduces taxable income now)
- **Deferrability** — postpone taxes until later (usually retirement, when you're in a lower bracket)

**Power of Tax-Deferred Savings — $458/month contribution example:**

| | Non-Registered (No Tax Advantage) | RRSP/TFSA (Tax Deferred/Free) |
|---|---|---|
| 10 Years | $74,300 | $87,600 |
| 20 Years | $205,400 | $295,100 |
| 30 Years | $436,900 | $786,200 |
| 40 Years | $845,700 | $1,948,900 |

**Registered Plans Comparison (Canada):**

| Feature | RRSP | TFSA |
|---|---|---|
| Annual Limit (2023) | $30,780 of prior year earned income | $6,500/year (max $88,000 total if 18+ since 2009) |
| 2024 RRSP Limit | $31,560 | — |
| Deductibility | ✅ Deductible | ❌ Not deductible |
| Carry Forward | ✅ Yes | ✅ Yes |
| Earnings | Tax-Deferred | **Tax-Free** |
| Withdrawals | Taxable (withholding applies) | Tax-Free |
| Max Age for Contribution | 71 | No maximum |
| Re-contribution of Withdrawals | ❌ No (except HBP/LLP) | ✅ Yes (next calendar year) |

**RRSP High Cost of Waiting Example ($5,000/year at 9%):**
- Individual A: Contributes ages 22–29 only (total: $40,000 contributed)
  - **Accumulation at 65: $1,542,700**
- Individual B: Contributes ages 30–65 (total: $180,000 contributed)
  - **Accumulation at 65: $1,412,450**
- Starting 8 years earlier with $140,000 LESS contributed = $130,250 MORE at retirement

### 7. BECOME AN OWNER, NOT A LOANER

> "Banks pay you a low rate to hold your money — then invest it at a higher rate. You're the loaner. They're the owner."

**The Guaranteed Loss:**
- Deposit $10,000 at 1% interest
- Earn $100 interest, pay $25 in taxes (25% rate) = $75 net
- Net balance: $10,075
- At 4% inflation: buying power drops to **$9,688**
- **You actually LOST purchasing power in a "safe" savings account**

**The Broken Three-Legged Stool:**
Traditional retirement depended on:
1. Government benefits (CPP/OAS) — increasingly uncertain
2. Company pensions — mostly gone
3. Personal savings — up to YOU

> "Simply put, it's up to you to fund your retirement."

**The Answer: Equity Investments (Stock Market)**
- Savings accounts and GICs won't keep pace with inflation
- Equity investments give you the potential for higher returns
- Risk exists, but so does reward
- To get a higher rate of return, you must become an **owner** in the economy

**30-Year Performance Comparison ($10,000 invested 1992–2022):**

| S&P/TSX Total Return Index | 8.7% | $121,257 |
| MSCI World Index | 8.3% | $109,368 |
| 5-Year GIC | 3.2% | $25,623 |
| Consumer Price Index (inflation) | 2.0% | $18,076 |

### INVEST WITH PROFESSIONAL MANAGEMENT (Mutual Funds)

**What Is a Mutual Fund?**
A pool of money from many investors, managed by professional money managers who invest across many companies and industries.

**The Three D's of Investing:**

1. **Discipline** — Stay invested through all market conditions. Missing even a handful of the best market days dramatically reduces long-term returns. Don't try to time the market.

2. **Diversification** — No single perfect investment exists. Balance a variety of investments to reduce risk and smooth out returns. Diversification does not guarantee profit or protect against loss.

3. **Dollar-Cost Averaging (DCA)** — Invest a fixed amount every month, regardless of market conditions. You automatically buy MORE units when prices are low and FEWER when prices are high. The average cost per unit drops over time.

**DCA Example — $100/month over 6 months:**

| | Investor A (Rising Market) | Investor B (Fluctuating Market) |
|---|---|---|
| Share prices | $10 → $20 (rising) | $10 → $2 → $10 (dip + recovery) |
| Total invested | $600 | $600 |
| Units accumulated | 42.28 | **125.95** |
| Average cost/unit | $14.19 | **$4.76** |

> Investor B accumulated 3x more units despite the market going DOWN mid-period. That's dollar-cost averaging at work.
