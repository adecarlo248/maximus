## Rod Norville 1APP sales bot prompt revision

- Reviewed Rod's Claude feedback against the existing 1APP sales-line prompt and accepted the five proposed improvements with safeguards.
- Reduced cold-prospect SMS intake to callback time first, then missing name and business name; email is requested only when the prospect asks for emailed information or books a demo requiring it. Phone and business address are not re-requested during normal SMS callback booking.
- Added honest responses for “Who is this?”, “What's this about?”, “Is this a bot?”, and “How did you get my number?” The public-listing answer may be used only when the campaign record verifies that source.
- Added workflow requirements to alert Rod immediately on the first genuine human reply and again when a callback time is captured, with a concise prospect summary.
- Added optional trade-specific seasonal examples while prohibiting unverified assumptions about a prospect's services.
- Preserved exact-match suppression for canned phone replies and the 1APP Sprint, qualification, pricing, billing, compliance, objection, voice-call, and guardrail content.
- Final prompt is 1,988 words and stored at `1app/Rod-Norville-Sales-Line-AI-Prompt.md`.

## Dot competitive objection

- A 1APP prospect replied that Dot already handles their admin. Verified the likely product as OpenAI Dots, an always-on general work agent that can work across connected tools, cloud/local computers, supported plugins, and Slack/Teams.
- Position 1APP as complementary rather than a replacement: Dot can manage internal knowledge/admin work, while 1APP runs customer-facing lead capture, missed-call recovery, SMS/email follow-up, CRM pipeline, booking, reminders, reviews, and approved contact reactivation.
- Never promise a direct Dot-to-1APP integration. Possible handoffs through shared email/calendar, Slack/Teams, supported plugins, APIs, webhooks, or an automation bridge require a technical review of the prospect's actual setup.
- Created `1app/rod-sales-system/Dot-vs-1APP-Objection-Card.md` with Rod's SMS reply, discovery questions, reactivation angle, integration language, and guardrails.

## CrewClock forgotten-punch safeguards

- Added configurable company attendance reminders for regular workdays, start/end times, clock-in and clock-out grace periods, timezone, and a long-open-shift threshold.
- Employees now receive persistent in-app reminders for a missed clock-in, a likely forgotten clock-out, or an unusually long shift. A missed-clock-in reminder can be dismissed as “Not working today,” and that exception is visible to managers.
- Managers now see live attendance exceptions in the team roster. Long shifts are flagged for payroll review when the employee clocks out.
- CrewClock never fabricates or automatically closes punches; reminders and flags require the employee or manager to review the actual record.
- Added additive D1 migration `0012_attendance_reminders.sql`, six attendance-rule tests, and updated privacy, terms, and app-store copy.
- Verified 58 tests, TypeScript, production build, and live reminder UI/legal assets. Published at `https://1app-crewclock.higgsfield.app` in CrewClock commit `d418767`.
