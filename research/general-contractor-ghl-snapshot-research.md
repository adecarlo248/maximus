Their CRM is typically a combination of email threads, paper estimates, spreadsheets, and memory. Every missed follow-up on a bid is $10,000–$150,000+ walking out the door.

The opportunity: Build the most complete, GC-specific GHL snapshot on the market — one that speaks the language of job sites, uses the right terminology (scope of work, change order, punch list, draw schedule, RFI, lien waiver), and solves the actual operational pain GCs face daily.

**What this snapshot needs to be:** A fully operational, plug-and-play GHL setup that a GC can activate and run within 48 hours — covering lead intake, estimate follow-up, contract signing reminders, project milestone communication, change order approvals, post-project referral capture, and seasonal campaigns.

## PART 1: GC BUSINESS PAIN POINTS (DEEP DIVE)

### 1.1 The Estimate-to-Close Gap

**The problem:** GCs invest significant time walking job sites, measuring, and writing detailed scopes of work. A quality estimate takes 2–8 hours. The average residential GC sends 5–15 estimates per month. Closing rate without follow-up: 20–30%. With systematic follow-up: 40–55%.

**What GCs actually do:** Send the estimate by email, wait, and hope. When the prospect doesn't call back within a week, the GC mentally marks it as dead. In reality, 60–70% of prospects who don't respond immediately are still shopping — they're comparing quotes, talking to their spouse, or waiting to see if they can get financing.

**The fix in GHL:** Automated follow-up sequence starting 24 hours after estimate delivery. Multi-touch: email + SMS. Tone must be professional and project-oriented, never pushy. GCs hate sounding like used car salespeople. The messaging needs to frame follow-up as "making sure you have everything you need to make your decision."

**Pain scale: 9/10** — The most impactful automation for immediate ROI.

---

### 1.2 Change Order Disputes

**The problem:** Change orders (COs) are where GC relationships go bad. A client approves verbal changes during construction — "while you're at it, let's move that outlet" — then disputes the cost on the invoice. Without documented approval, the GC either absorbs the cost or damages the client relationship.

**Industry standard:** Every CO should have: description of work, materials cost, labor cost, schedule impact (days added to timeline), and explicit client sign-off before work begins.

**The fix in GHL:** A change order request workflow that triggers when the GC submits a CO from the field. Sends client an SMS/email with CO details and a one-click approval link. If no response in 24 hours, auto-escalates with a second notification. Work doesn't start until the system logs approval. This alone can protect $5,000–$50,000/year in disputed charges.

**Pain scale: 8/10**

---

### 1.3 Subcontractor Coordination

**The problem:** GCs coordinate 5–15 subcontractors (electrical, plumbing, HVAC, drywall, tile, etc.) per project. Scheduling a sub for the wrong day when the rough framing isn't done yet wastes everyone's time and creates hostile relationships. Most GCs manage this with phone calls and texts.

**What GCs need:** A system that sends subs automated scheduling confirmations, day-before reminders, and allows the GC to send a "site ready" trigger when the preceding trade is done. This is partly a project management problem, but GHL can handle the communication layer.

**The fix in GHL:** Sub-facing workflows. Create a contact type "Subcontractor" with custom fields (trade type, license #, insurance expiry, contact). Automated scheduling confirmation when the GC adds a job to their calendar. Day-before reminder SMS. "Site is ready for you" trigger text.

**Pain scale: 7/10**

---

### 1.4 Cash Flow on Milestone/Draw Schedules

**The problem:** Residential construction contracts typically structure payment as a draw schedule tied to project milestones: 
- **Draw 1:** Deposit / contract signing (10–20%)
- **Draw 2:** Framing complete (20–25%)
- **Draw 3:** Rough-in (mechanical/electrical/plumbing) complete (20–25%)
- **Draw 4:** Drywall / substantial completion (20–25%)
- **Draw 5:** Final punch list / certificate of occupancy (10–15%)

GCs routinely forget to invoice on milestone completion — especially mid-project when they're running 3–5 jobs simultaneously. A $150,000 job with a 5-draw schedule means the GC might be carrying $30,000–$50,000 in unbilled work.

**The fix in GHL:** Pipeline stages that trigger payment reminder workflows. When the GC moves a deal to "Rough-In Complete," an automatic invoice reminder goes to the client and the GC gets a task to submit the draw request.

**Pain scale: 8/10**

---

### 1.5 Permit Delays and Client Communication

**The problem:** Permits in most jurisdictions take 2–6 weeks for residential, 6–16 weeks for commercial. Clients have no idea how the permit process works. They think the GC is dragging their feet. The GC gets daily "where are we?" calls that consume 30–60 minutes of admin time per active project.

**The fix in GHL:** Project status update automation. Weekly "Project Status Update" email/SMS that tells the client exactly where they are in the process, what's happening this week, and what the next milestone is. Even during permit wait: "Your permit is in review. Expected approval: [date]. We're scheduling subs now so we're ready to mobilize immediately on approval."

**Pain scale: 7/10**

---

### 1.6 Referral Capture Failure

**The problem:** GCs live and die on referrals. A satisfied client who refers two friends is worth $300,000–$600,000 in lifetime client value. But most GCs never systematically ask for referrals. They assume happy clients will spread the word. Sometimes they do. Usually they don't — because no one asked.

**The fix in GHL:** Post-project referral campaign. 3–7 days after final payment: "How did we do?" survey. On 5-star response: automatic referral request with a personalized message. On 4-star or below: route to the GC owner for a personal call. This single workflow can generate 2–5 additional projects per year per client.

**Pain scale: 9/10**

---

### 1.7 Long Sales Cycles on Large Projects

**The problem:** A $500K kitchen-and-addition project might have a 3–6 month decision cycle. The prospect meetings the GC in January, gets a proposal in February, and signs in June. Without a long-nurture system, the GC goes radio silent after the proposal and loses to the competitor who stayed top of mind.

**The fix in GHL:** Long-nurture sequence for high-value prospects. Bi-monthly educational content during the decision window — not "are you ready to sign" messages but value-add content: "6 Things to Ask Before You Sign a Renovation Contract," "How Draw Schedules Protect You as a Homeowner," etc.

**Pain scale: 7/10**

---

### 1.8 Online Reviews and Reputation

**The problem:** GCs need Google reviews to rank locally. Most have fewer than 20 reviews. Their competitors with 80+ reviews win the Google Map Pack. But GCs hate asking for reviews — it feels awkward and unprofessional.

**The fix in GHL:** Automated post-project review request. Timed to arrive when the client's emotional high is at its peak — typically 1–2 days after the crew clears the job site and the client sees the finished product for the first time. Direct link to Google review page. No asking — just a QR code text or direct link.

**Pain scale: 8/10**

## PART 4: WORKFLOWS (COMPLETE AUTOMATION BUILD SPECS)

### 4.1 Lead Intake Workflow

**Trigger:** Any form submission, missed call, Facebook Lead Ad, or Google LSA lead

**Sequence:**
1. **Immediate (0 min):** Lead created in system, tagged with source (Google / Referral / Houzz / Angi / Facebook / Website / Word of Mouth)
2. **Immediate:** Auto-SMS to lead:
   > "Hi [First Name]! This is [GC Name] from [Company]. Thanks for reaching out about your [project type if captured, else "project"]. I'll be in touch shortly to learn more about what you're working on. What's a good time to connect?"
3. **Immediate:** Internal task created for GC/team: "New lead — call within 2 hours"
4. **If no response in 2 hours:** Second SMS:
   > "Just following up, [First Name]. Happy to answer any questions or schedule a quick call. When works for you?"
5. **If no response in 24 hours:** Email with booking link:
   > Subject: "Let's discuss your [project type] — pick a time that works"
6. **If no contact after 72 hours:** Flag to GC owner for personal outreach or assign to "Attempted Contact" stage

**Custom Fields Captured:**
- Project type (dropdown: Remodel / Addition / New Build / Commercial / Other)
- Estimated budget range
- Desired start date
- Property address / zip code
- How did you hear about us?

---

### 4.2 Estimate Follow-Up Workflow

**Trigger:** Contact moved to "Estimate Sent" stage

**Sequence:**
1. **Day 1 (24 hrs after estimate delivery):** SMS:
   > "Hi [First Name], just checking in — did you have a chance to review the estimate for your [project type]? Happy to walk through any part of it or answer questions."

2. **Day 3:** Email:
   > Subject: "Questions about your [project type] estimate?"
   > Body: "Hi [First Name], I wanted to follow up on the estimate I sent over. I know renovation decisions take time, and I want to make sure you have everything you need to feel confident moving forward. [3–4 bullet points of value-add: what's included, what sets us apart, our warranty, etc.] Reply here or call me at [phone]."

3. **Day 7:** SMS:
   > "Hi [First Name], still happy to answer any questions about the [project type] proposal. We're booking [season] projects now — let me know if you'd like to lock in your spot."

4. **Day 14:** Personal-feeling email:
   > Subject: "Still thinking it over?"
   > Body: "Hi [First Name], I don't want to be a pest, but I know a lot of folks are comparing estimates right now and sometimes just need one or two questions answered. If there's anything in the scope of work you'd like to clarify, or if the timing or budget needs to shift, I'm flexible. Would love to earn your business."

5. **Day 30:** Final email before moving to long-nurture:
   > "Hi [First Name], I'll step back after this — just wanted to make sure you know we're still here if you move forward with this project. [Include a recent project photo or testimonial.] Wishing you all the best."

6. **Day 30+:** Move to 6-month long-nurture (monthly value-add emails)

**Deal outcome branches:**
- If "Won": Move to Deposit Received stage → trigger construction sequence
- If "Lost": Move to Closed-Lost → trigger re-engagement nurture

### 4.3 Contract Signing Reminder Workflow

**Trigger:** Contact moved to "Contract Sent" stage

**Sequence:**
1. **Day 1:** Email with contract link (Pandadoc/DocuSign integration or GHL PDF link)
2. **Day 2 (24 hrs):** SMS:
   > "Hi [First Name], just wanted to confirm you received the contract for your [project type]. Let me know if you have questions before signing."
3. **Day 4:** Final reminder:
   > "Hi [First Name], wanted to follow up on the contract. Once signed and deposit received, I can officially lock in your start date and begin lining up the crew. Let me know if anything needs to be adjusted."
4. **Day 7 (no signature):** Internal task — GC personal call

### 4.4 Change Order Approval Workflow

**Trigger:** GC submits a "Change Order Request" form (internal form)

**Change Order Form Fields:**
- Client name / project
- Change order number (auto-incremented)
- Description of additional scope
- Material cost
- Labor cost
- Total CO amount
- Schedule impact (# of additional days)
- GC signature / authorization

**Automation:**
1. **Immediate:** Email to client:
   > Subject: "Change Order #[CO#] — [Project Name] — Approval Required"
   > Body: "Hi [First Name], during construction we identified an opportunity/requirement to modify the original scope. Here are the details:
   > **Work Description:** [CO description]
   > **Additional Cost:** $[amount]
   > **Schedule Impact:** [X] additional business days
   > **Your approval is required before we proceed with this work.**
   > [APPROVE / DECLINE button links]"

2. **Immediate:** SMS to client:
   > "Hi [First Name], we've sent you a Change Order request for your [project type]. Please check your email and approve or decline before we proceed. Total: $[amount]."

3. **If no response in 24 hours:** Follow-up SMS:
   > "Hi [First Name], just following up on Change Order #[CO#]. We need your approval before we can continue with this portion of the work."

4. **On approval:** Client response logged, work authorized, task created for GC: "CO #[CO#] approved — proceed."

5. **On decline:** Task created for GC: "CO #[CO#] declined — call client to discuss"

**CO Tracking Custom Field:** Store all CO numbers, amounts, and status on the contact record

### 4.5 Project Milestone / Weekly Update Workflow

**Trigger:** Contact moved to "Construction Active" stage

**Sequence (weekly recurring):**

**Week 1:**
> "Hi [First Name], your project is officially underway! This week we're focused on [current phase — e.g., demolition, framing prep]. Here's what to expect: [2–3 sentences]. Our next milestone is [milestone name] — we'll keep you updated. Questions? Reply here or call [phone]."

**Week 2+:** Continue weekly updates triggered manually or via tag update by GC team. Template text for each major phase:

- **Demolition week:** Focus on protecting adjacent spaces, dumpster placement, dust management
- **Rough framing:** Walls going up, layout confirmed, feel of the space emerging
- **Rough-in (MEP):** Electrical, plumbing, HVAC behind the walls — inspections scheduled
- **Insulation / drywall:** Walls closing up, space taking shape
- **Finishes:** The fun phase — cabinets, flooring, tile, paint
- **Punch list:** Final walkthrough, correcting any items, cleaning

**Why this matters:** Proactive communication prevents "check-in" calls that waste 30+ min/day.

4. **If no payment in 72 hours:** Internal task — GC owner call

**Note:** GC must update payment amounts as custom fields on each deal before triggering stages.

## PART 8: COMMERCIAL / TENANT IMPROVEMENT WORKFLOW

### 8.1 Commercial TI Differences vs. Residential

| Factor | Residential | Commercial TI |
|--------|-------------|---------------|
| Decision maker | Homeowner (1–2 people) | Tenant + Landlord + Property Manager |
| Timeline | 2–8 weeks decision, 4–24 weeks construction | 4–16 weeks bid/award, 8–24 weeks construction |
| Contract type | Fixed price, lump sum | Fixed price, GMP, or Cost-Plus |
| Permit authority | Local building department | Local + fire marshal + health dept (for food service) |
| Inspections | Standard municipal | May include special fire suppression, health dept (restaurants) |
| Lien waivers | Common | Required by landlord/lender at each draw |
| Substantial completion | Homeowner walkthrough | Architect issues SC certificate |
| Warranty | Standard 1-year workmanship | Contract-specified, often 1–2 years |

### 8.2 Commercial TI Lead Sources

1. **Commercial real estate brokers** — Tenants signing new leases often need TI immediately
2. **Property management companies** — Landlords managing buildout allowances
3. **Architects / interior designers** — Design-bid-build GC selection
4. **Direct from tenant (restaurant, retail, medical office)** — Referrals and repeat business
5. **GC's existing network** — Past commercial clients expanding

## APPENDIX A: GLOSSARY OF GC INDUSTRY TERMS (FOR TEMPLATE USE)

| Term | Definition | Use in Templates |
|------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Scope of Work** | Detailed description of what work will be done | "per the scope of work" |
| **Change Order (CO)** | Written modification to original contract scope | "Change Order #[CO#]" |
| **Draw Schedule** | Pre-agreed payment milestones tied to construction progress | "per your draw schedule" |
| **RFI** | Request for Information — formal question to architect/engineer | Commercial TI context |
| **Submittal** | Product data/shop drawings submitted for architect approval | Commercial TI context |
| **Punch List** | List of final items to correct before substantial completion | "punch list walkthrough" |
| **Substantial Completion** | Project is complete enough for owner occupancy/use | "SC letter" |
| **Certificate of Occupancy (CO)** | Municipal approval to occupy the building | "CO received" |
| **Lien Waiver** | Legal document releasing contractor's right to file lien | "conditional/unconditional lien waiver" |
| **Notice to Proceed (NTP)** | Written authorization to begin work | Commercial context |
| **Rough-In** | Mechanical/electrical/plumbing installed but not finished | "MEP rough-in complete" |
| **GMP** | Guaranteed Maximum Price — contract type with cost ceiling | New build/commercial context |
| **Design-Build** | GC provides both design and construction services | Lead qualification tag |
| **Mobilization** | Moving equipment and crew onto job site | "mobilize upon permit approval" |
| **Closeout** | Final documentation, as-builts, warranties, O&M manuals | Commercial context |
| **As-Builts** | Drawings showing what was actually built vs. designed | Commercial handover |
| **O&M Manuals** | Operations and Maintenance documentation | Commercial handover |
| **WSIB / WCB** | Workers compensation board clearance (Canada) | Sub verification |
| **COI** | Certificate of Insurance | Sub verification |
