## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The chimney and fireplace vertical is a high-value, underserved niche in the home services space. Most operators run entirely on phone calls, paper schedules, and word of mouth. They are acutely seasonal, heavily dependent on trust, and sitting on repeat revenue they never systematically capture.

**The GHL opportunity:** No dominant, well-built snapshot exists for this trade. Most chimney operators have zero CRM, no automated follow-up, no review system, and no structured WETT inspection workflow. A purpose-built 1app snapshot can be the category killer in this space.

**Canadian differentiation:** WETT (Wood Energy Technology Transfer) inspection requirements tied to real estate transactions and insurance renewals create a regulatory-driven lead pipeline that most chimney operators don't actively market. This is a massive opportunity unique to the Canadian market.

## SECTION 2: BUSINESS PAIN POINTS (Deep Dive)

### Pain Point #1: Extreme Seasonality — The September Crunch
- **80% of revenue in 8–12 weeks** (September through November in Canada)
- Phones ring off the hook in October; operators turn away work
- February–August: dead zone — most have no structured off-season lead capture
- No system to spread demand or capture shoulder-season work
- Operators scramble every August to remind people to book — no automation does it for them

**1app Snapshot Solution:** Fall Season Campaign workflow launches Aug 15 automatically. Annual sweep reminder sequences re-engage last year's clients in July. Pipeline data shows upcoming appointment gaps.

---

### Pain Point #2: WETT Inspection Demand — Unmanaged Goldmine
- **WETT (Wood Energy Technology Transfer) certification** is the Canadian standard for wood-burning appliance inspection
- Required by law or lender policy in most Canadian provinces when:
  - Selling a home with a wood-burning appliance (fireplace, stove, insert)
  - Applying for home insurance (many insurers require WETT for new policies)
  - Installing a new wood-burning appliance
  - Qualifying for a mortgage on a home with wood-burning equipment
- **WETT Site Basic:** visual inspection of accessible components (~$200–$300)
- **WETT Comprehensive:** internal inspection, often including camera inspection of flue ($300–$450+)
- Real estate agents generate MASSIVE referral volume — every home sale with a wood-burning appliance triggers a WETT requirement
- Most chimney companies have no structured referral system for realtors
- No one has built an automated WETT inspection booking and follow-up flow

**1app Snapshot Solution:** Dedicated WETT booking funnel, real estate agent referral program with automated follow-up, WETT-specific landing page, and post-inspection upsell flow for recommended repairs.

---

### Pain Point #3: No Recurring Revenue System
- Annual chimney cleaning is the definition of a recurring service — but most operators have no mechanism to ensure customers come back
- No annual reminder system
- Customers book once, forget to call back, and the operator loses the relationship
- NFPA 211 standard recommends annual chimney inspection — operators aren't leveraging this
- Creosote buildup (Stage 1, 2, 3) requires annual maintenance — customers don't self-educate without prompting

**1app Snapshot Solution:** Annual sweep reminder campaign triggers 11 months after last service. Automated postcard-style SMS/email sequence. "Your chimney is due for its annual sweep" campaign with booking link.

---

### Pain Point #4: Low Average Ticket — Repair Upsell Gap
- A $175 chimney sweep is fine, but a cracked firebox, damaged smoke chamber, or failed flue liner is a $1,500–$5,000 repair job
- Operators sweep the chimney, note issues verbally, and often never follow up
- No structured estimate-to-close pipeline for repair work
- Customers need multiple touches to approve large-ticket repairs — operators do one call and give up

**1app Snapshot Solution:** Post-sweep repair estimate pipeline. Automated follow-up sequence for outstanding estimates: "Your chimney sweep found [issue] — here's what happens if you don't address it." 3-touch sequence over 10 days.

---

### Pain Point #5: Zero Review Capture Strategy
- Chimney sweeps generate enormous goodwill — customers are relieved and grateful
- Almost nobody asks for reviews systematically
- Google reviews are the #1 driver of chimney business discovery
- Operators sit on a gold mine of satisfied customers who've never been asked

**1app Snapshot Solution:** Post-service review request SMS fires 2 hours after job marked complete. 72-hour follow-up if no review link clicked. 5-star review flow to Google/HomeStars.

---

### Pain Point #6: Emergency Call Chaos
- Chimney fires and carbon monoxide concerns create emergency calls
- No system to triage, dispatch, or follow up on emergency work
- Emergency jobs often generate referrals and repeat business — never captured

**1app Snapshot Solution:** Emergency intake form with auto-dispatch SMS to tech. Post-emergency follow-up campaign with safety tips and next-year booking prompt.

---

### Pain Point #7: Insurance Company & Adjuster Referrals
- After a chimney fire, insurance adjusters order chimney inspections and restoration
- Most chimney companies have zero system to cultivate adjuster relationships
- No B2B pipeline management for insurance referrals

**1app Snapshot Solution:** Insurance adjuster contact database with quarterly check-in campaign. Referral tracking tags in CRM.

## SECTION 9: WETT INSPECTION WORKFLOW — CANADA DEEP DIVE

### Why WETT is the 1app Snapshot's Biggest Differentiator

WETT Inc. (Wood Energy Technology Transfer) is the non-profit organization that provides training and certification for professionals who install, service, and inspect wood energy systems in Canada. WETT certification is recognized across all Canadian provinces and is the standard referenced by:

- Real estate lawyers closing on homes with wood-burning appliances
- Mortgage lenders (especially CMHC-insured mortgages)
- Home insurance companies underwriting policies with wood-burning appliances
- Municipal building departments for new appliance installations
- Home inspectors who note the absence of a WETT report

### WETT Inspection Types

**WETT Site Basic Inspection:**
- Visual inspection of accessible components only
- Cannot include dismantling or invasive examination
- Covers: appliance make/model/clearances, connector and chimney system visual assessment, exterior chimney visual
- Most commonly required for: home sales where appliance appears to be in good condition, insurance renewals
- Duration: 45–90 minutes
- Typical fee: $200–$300

**WETT Comprehensive Inspection:**
- All of Site Basic PLUS internal examination of flue
- Often includes camera inspection (Level 2 equivalent)
- Required when: deficiencies noted in Site Basic, appliance is older/unknown, pre-purchase of home with older appliances
- Duration: 90–150 minutes
- Typical fee: $350–$500+

### The Real Estate Pipeline — Every Home Sale is a Lead

When a Canadian home with a wood-burning appliance is listed for sale:

1. Realtor listing agent recommends WETT inspection (or buyer's agent requests it)
2. Chimney company gets referral (if they have a relationship with the realtor)
3. WETT inspection performed
4. If pass: Certificate issued, deal can close
5. If conditional/fail: Repairs recommended, timeline becomes critical

**The opportunity:** A single real estate brokerage office with 20 active agents could generate 10–30 WETT inspection referrals per month during peak real estate season (spring and fall). Most chimney companies have no structured relationship with any realtor.

### Real Estate Realtor Outreach Strategy

**Initial Contact (for GHL realtor database):**
- Source realtor lists from local boards, Zoocasa, Realtor.ca agent directory
- Tag by brokerage, market area, production volume
- Outreach sequence:

  2. SMS: "Hi [Name], sent you an email about WETT inspections — happy to be your go-to for fast turnaround. Takes real estate seriously."
  3. In-person: Drop off a "WETT Inspection Priority Card" (physical card with QR code to booking page)

**Ongoing Realtor Nurture:**
- Monthly: Market activity note + reminder of WETT service

## SECTION 10: FALL CAMPAIGN — PEAK DEMAND PLAYBOOK

### The Math Behind the Fall Campaign

A chimney company with 200 past customers who doesn't run a fall campaign can expect:
- ~30–40% return without any prompting (loyal customers)
- Leaving 60–70% of recurring revenue on the table

With a structured fall campaign (as outlined above):
- 55–70% return rate is achievable
- 200 customers × 70% return × $200 avg ticket = $28,000 from one campaign
- Incremental revenue from a 3-SMS campaign: $5,600–$14,000 per season

## APPENDIX: INDUSTRY GLOSSARY (For Template Accuracy)

| Term | Definition | Use in Templates |
|---|---|---|
| **Flue** | The interior channel of a chimney that carries combustion gases out | "Your flue was inspected and found to be in good/poor condition" |
| **Flue liner** | Material lining the interior of the flue (terra cotta, stainless steel, HeatShield®) | "Your terra cotta flue liner shows signs of deterioration" |
| **Firebox** | The combustion chamber where wood is burned | "Cracked firebricks in your firebox require repointing" |
| **Smoke chamber** | The area above the firebox that compresses and directs smoke into the flue | "Your smoke chamber shows corbelling damage" |
| **Damper** | Adjustable plate that controls airflow into the flue | "Your throat damper was found to be corroded/stuck" |
| **Chimney crown** | Concrete or mortar cap at the top of a masonry chimney that sheds water | "Your crown has cracked, allowing water to penetrate the flue" |
| **Chimney cap** | Metal cap with mesh that sits over the flue opening | "We recommend installing a stainless steel chimney cap to prevent animal entry and water damage" |
| **Flashing** | Metal strip sealing the chimney/roof junction | "Your step flashing shows separation and is allowing water infiltration" |
| **Creosote** | Combustion byproduct that accumulates in the flue; Stage 1 (dusty), Stage 2 (tar-like), Stage 3 (glazed) | "Stage 2 creosote was found in your flue — this requires chemical treatment or professional removal" |
| **Spalling** | Brick surface flaking or breaking away due to moisture cycling | "The exterior of your chimney shows significant spalling — a sign of moisture damage" |
| **Tuckpointing** | Re-applying mortar to deteriorated joints between bricks | "We recommend tuckpointing the mortar joints on the north face of your chimney" |
| **Level 1 Inspection** | Visual inspection of accessible areas — NFPA 211 standard, annual minimum | "Your annual sweep includes a Level 1 inspection" |
| **Level 2 Inspection** | All of Level 1 + video scan of internal flue — required for real estate transactions | "A Level 2 inspection is required for your home sale" |
| **Level 3 Inspection** | All of Level 2 + destructive examination as needed — for serious damage assessment | "Given the chimney fire, a Level 3 inspection is required before the system can be used again" |
| **WETT** | Wood Energy Technology Transfer — Canadian certification for wood-burning appliance inspection | "Our WETT-certified inspector will provide a written report" |
| **WETT Site Basic** | WETT inspection of accessible components only | "Your WETT Site Basic inspection includes..." |

| **Insert** | Fireplace insert — a self-contained unit installed into an existing masonry or factory-built fireplace | "We install gas and wood-burning fireplace inserts" |
| **EPA-certified** | Wood stove meets EPA Phase 2 emission standards (required for new stoves sold in Canada) | "We install only EPA-certified wood stoves" |
| **HeatShield®** | Proprietary ceramic resurfacing product for damaged flue liners | "HeatShield® restores your damaged flue liner without full relining" |
| **Carbon monoxide** | Odourless toxic gas produced by incomplete combustion | "A blocked or damaged flue can allow CO to enter your living space" |
| **Draft** | Upward airflow through the chimney that removes combustion gases | "Poor draft can indicate an obstruction, negative air pressure, or flue sizing issue" |
