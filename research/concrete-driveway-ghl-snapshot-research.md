## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Concrete and driveway contractors are one of the highest-ROI niches for a GHL SaaS agency. Average job values run $3,000–$25,000+ (driveway replacement, stamped concrete patios, commercial parking lots), decision cycles are short (7–21 days for residential, 30–90 for commercial), and the niche is dramatically underserved by CRM automation. Most operators are owner-operators running 1–8 crews, managing leads via missed calls, paper estimates, and their own memory.

The concrete/driveway niche covers distinct service lines that each require different automation logic:

| Service | Avg. Ticket (CDN) | Decision Cycle | Seasonality |
|---|---|---|---|
| Driveway replacement (asphalt) | $5,000–$12,000 | 7–14 days | May–Oct |
| Driveway replacement (concrete) | $8,000–$20,000 | 10–21 days | May–Sep |
| Interlock / paving stone driveway | $12,000–$35,000 | 14–30 days | Apr–Oct |
| Stamped concrete patio | $10,000–$40,000 | 14–30 days | Apr–Sep |
| Concrete flatwork (slab, walkway, steps) | $3,000–$12,000 | 7–14 days | Apr–Oct |
| Crack repair / resurfacing | $500–$3,000 | 1–7 days | Apr–Nov |
| Annual sealing (existing clients) | $300–$800 | Same-day booking | May–Jun, Sep–Oct |
| Commercial parking lot (asphalt overlay) | $15,000–$150,000+ | 30–90 days | May–Sep |

**The opportunity:** No GHL snapshot on the market fully addresses this niche with seasonal logic, split pipelines per service type, neighbour campaigns, sealing upsell automation, or contractor-specific language. 1app can own this space.

## 2. INDUSTRY PROFILE & BUSINESS PAIN POINTS

### 2.1 Who These Contractors Are

- **Typical operator:** Owner-operator or small crew (2–15 employees)
- **Revenue range:** $200K–$3M/year
- **Location:** Suburban and rural Ontario, Quebec, Prairie provinces (Canada); Northeast/Midwest US
- **Mix:** Residential 60–80%, commercial 20–40%
- **Tech stack:** Typically zero CRM. Maybe Google Sheets, an old estimate template, and a Facebook page.
- **Primary concerns:** Staying booked through the short season, not losing leads to competitors, getting more referrals, getting paid faster

### 2.2 Core Business Pain Points

#### PAIN 1: Short Seasonal Window = Revenue Compression
- In Canada/Northern US, the **concrete pour season** runs roughly **May 1 – October 15**
- Asphalt paving season: **April 15 – November 1** (asphalt is more forgiving but still temperature-dependent)
- Concrete flatwork has strict temperature requirements: ambient temp 10°C–32°C, no pour during freeze/thaw, 28-day cure window before winter
- Interlock work can push to late October/early November (no pour, just setting pavers)
- **Impact:** Every missed lead during peak season is gone forever. A missed call in June at 4:30pm = job goes to the competitor who answered.

#### PAIN 2: Quote-to-Close Cycle Gets Interrupted
- Most contractors quote verbally or via email PDF
- No follow-up system = quotes die in inboxes
- The contractor "forgets" to follow up; the homeowner shops 3 other companies
- **Industry stat:** The contractor who follows up 3+ times closes 80% more than those who don't follow up at all

#### PAIN 3: Speed-to-Lead Failure
- Concrete/driveway leads go cold within **2–4 hours** — especially in spring/summer when homeowners are in planning mode and want to book before the good dates are gone
- Most operators check messages at end of day or next morning
- Google LSA, Kijiji, and Facebook lead ads fire 24/7 — the call or text that doesn't get answered goes to the next guy in the search results

#### PAIN 4: No Review Capture System
- Most satisfied customers never leave a review because they're never asked properly
- Contractors with 50+ Google reviews dominate local packs
- The "ask at job completion" fails 90% of the time if not automated
- **Impact:** More reviews = better GMB ranking = more inbound leads

#### PAIN 5: No Recurring Revenue on Past Clients
- Concrete sealer must be applied every 2–4 years (penetrating sealers: 3–5 years; film-forming sealers: 1–3 years)
- Asphalt should be seal-coated every 2–3 years
- Interlock joint sand replacement: every 3–7 years
- **Zero** contractors are running automated campaigns to their past client lists for annual sealing upsells

#### PAIN 6: Estimate Accuracy and Liability
- Quoting without site visit = inaccurate measure of square footage, grade, access, drainage slope
- Contractors lose money on jobs that were underquoted
- A structured booking flow (mandatory site visit before any estimate) protects margins

#### PAIN 7: Permit and Engineering Complexity on Larger Jobs
- Commercial parking lots, foundation work, and large residential flatwork projects require permits from the municipality
- Delay in permit = delay in start date = tight season gets tighter
- Contractors need a way to track where permits are in the pipeline

#### PAIN 8: No Neighbour Monetization
- After a driveway install on a street, every neighbour is a warm lead — they watched the crew work for 1–3 days
- Nobody systematically captures and follows up on street-level neighbours

#### PAIN 9: Financing Awareness Gap
- Driveway replacement ($8K–$20K) is a significant purchase
- Contractors who mention financing options in follow-up close 25–40% more deals
- Nobody has a workflow that introduces financing at the right moment (after estimate sent, if no response in 48 hours)

#### PAIN 10: Weather Windows Create Chaos
- A rain forecast kills a pour day and shuffles the whole schedule
- When concrete crews get rained out, they need to rebook — leads booked for that week need to be pushed
- No system to mass-SMS clients about rescheduled site visits

## 4. PIPELINE ARCHITECTURE

The concrete/driveway snapshot should use **multiple specialized pipelines** rather than one generic pipeline. This prevents job types from getting mixed, keeps reporting clean, and allows different automations per service type.

### 4.1 Pipeline 1: Residential Driveway (Primary)

Use for: Driveway replacement, new driveway install, asphalt or concrete driveway

## 6. LEAD SOURCES & ACQUISITION STRATEGY

### 6.1 Google Local Services Ads (LSA) — PRIMARY

**Why it's #1:** Concrete and driveway is a Google-first search category. When someone's driveway cracks after winter, they Google "driveway repair near me" or "concrete contractor Peterborough" — not Facebook, not Kijiji.

- LSA ads appear above paid search results
- Google-verified badge builds instant trust
- Pay-per-lead (not per click) — $20–$80 per lead depending on market
- Direct phone call tracking; GHL can log these automatically with call tracking numbers

**GHL Integration:** Use GHL call tracking numbers + SMS auto-reply when lead comes through LSA. The moment someone calls and it goes to voicemail, GHL fires the speed-to-lead SMS within 60 seconds.

### 6.2 Google Business Profile (GMB) Optimization

- Free inbound leads for contractors with strong review counts (50+)
- "Near me" searches rank GBP results heavily
- Photos of completed work (flatwork, stamped patios, driveways) dramatically increase profile performance
- **GHL snapshot should include:** Post-completion checklist that includes "Upload 2 photos to GMB"

### 6.3 Kijiji (Canada) / Facebook Marketplace

- Strong for residential driveway leads, especially rural and suburban Ontario
- Homeowners posting "Looking for concrete quotes" are high-intent, price-sensitive leads
- **Workflow:** Kijiji lead → GHL form → Speed-to-Lead sequence
- Kijiji Pro Ads can be managed via GHL integrations

### 6.4 Facebook/Instagram Ads

- Best for seasonal campaign bursts (spring urgency, fall sealing)
- Target: homeowners 35–65, $80K+ HHI, suburban postal codes
- Creative: Before/after driveway photos, crew in action, stamped concrete eye candy
- **GHL instant form integration:** Facebook Lead Ads → GHL contact → Speed-to-Lead fires immediately

### 6.5 Referral Program

- Concrete/driveway is a high-trust, referral-driven industry
- A past client who refers a job should get something: $100 gift card, credit toward sealing, Tim Hortons card
- **GHL workflow:** When a new contact is tagged "Referral — [Source Name]", send a thank-you and referral reward trigger to the referring client

### 6.6 Real Estate Agent Partnerships

- REAs need fast turnaround on driveways for listings: "we're listing in 3 weeks, needs a new driveway"
- High-value relationship: one realtor = 3–10 jobs per year
- **GHL workflow:** Dedicated pipeline stage for "Realtor Referral" → priority fast-track estimate sequence (2-day turnaround SLA)

### 6.7 Property Management Companies

- Commercial leads: apartment complexes, retail plazas, industrial parks
- Longer sales cycle but high contract values
- **GHL workflow:** Commercial pipeline + formal proposal sequence + multi-touch outreach over 30–90 days

### 6.8 Door Knocking / Yard Sign Program

- During and after every install, the crew puts a yard sign at the job site
- Owner or sales person door-knocks 5 houses in each direction
- **GHL workflow:** Quick-add contact from door knock → "Warm Neighbour Lead" tag → abbreviated estimate sequence

### 6.9 Home Shows & Trade Fairs

- Spring home shows (March–April) are huge for driveway/patio leads
- Display stamped concrete samples, interlock pavers
- **GHL workflow:** Event lead form → mass-import → speed-to-lead sequence with home show reference

## APPENDIX A: INDUSTRY GLOSSARY (Use in all client-facing copy)

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Flatwork | Horizontal concrete surfaces: driveways, sidewalks, patios, garage floors |
| Slab | Flat concrete pour; can be ground-supported or suspended |
| Rebar | Steel reinforcing bar embedded in concrete for tensile strength |
| Formwork | Temporary wooden or metal molds that hold concrete until cured |
| Pour | The act of placing fresh concrete mix into formwork |
| Cure | The chemical hardening process of concrete (28 days for full strength) |
| Sealer | Protective coating applied to cured concrete or asphalt |
| Asphalt overlay | New asphalt layer poured over existing deteriorated asphalt |
| Interlock / paving stone | Concrete or clay paving blocks set in a sand base |
| Stamped concrete | Decorative concrete with patterns impressed before curing |
| Exposed aggregate | Concrete where the surface is washed to expose embedded stone |
| Crack repair | Filling cracks with polyurethane or epoxy injection |
| Resurfacing | Thin overlay applied to damaged but structurally sound concrete |
| Joint sealing | Filling expansion joints to prevent water infiltration |
| Subbase | Crushed stone base layer beneath the concrete or asphalt |
| Compaction | Mechanically densifying the subbase layer for stability |
| Grade / slope | The angle of the surface for water drainage |
| Polymeric sand | Sand with binder for interlock joints; hardens when wet |
| Freeze-thaw cycle | The seasonal freezing and thawing that causes spalling and cracking |
| Spalling | Surface deterioration where concrete flakes or chips |
| Heaving | Frost-driven ground movement that lifts and cracks concrete |
