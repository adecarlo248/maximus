## SECTION 1: FLOORING INDUSTRY PAIN POINTS

### 1.1 The Multi-Quote Shopper Problem

Flooring buyers — especially residential — are habitual comparison shoppers. The average residential flooring customer gets **2.8 quotes** before making a decision. The problem is most flooring contractors have zero automated follow-up after the free in-home measure. The estimator walks out, sends a PDF quote via email, and then radio silence. The customer gets distracted, forgets, or gets seduced by the next quote that actually follows up.

**Pain point specifics:**
- No systematic speed-to-lead (same-day contact with an online inquiry is rare)
- Estimates sent as static PDFs with no follow-up sequence
- No mechanism to handle the "I'm still shopping around" objection via automation
- No urgency trigger tied to material lead times or promotional pricing windows
- Salespeople manually chasing quotes instead of the system doing it

### 1.2 The Sample Approval Black Hole

One of flooring's most unique friction points: the customer has to physically look at the floor sample in their home, under their lighting, next to their furniture. This means:

- Samples are dropped off (or mailed), then wait begins
- No automated check-in: "Did the [LVP oak] sample arrive? How does it look in your space?"
- Customers don't reply → contractor doesn't know if sample was received or rejected
- Jobs stall for 1–3 weeks in "sample review" purgatory
- No A/B nudge: "Looking for alternatives? Here are 3 more options in that price range"

This is a workflow most CRMs don't have at all. It's flooring-specific gold.

### 1.3 Material Lead Time Pressure

LVP, hardwood, custom tile, and imported products often have **4–12 week lead times**. The contractor knows this; the customer doesn't. When a job isn't closed quickly:

- Material availability changes
- Pricing from distributors fluctuates (especially with tariffs on Canadian/US imports)
- The customer's project gets delayed, sometimes missing their target date (reno completion, listing date, tenant move-in)

Automated lead time education creates urgency without being pushy: *"Heads up — the Hallmark Vienna LVP you liked typically runs 3–4 weeks on order. We'd want to lock in your selection by [date] to hit your target."*

### 1.4 The Subfloor Surprise

One of the most common customer complaints in the flooring industry: discovering subfloor issues (moisture, rot, uneven concrete, old adhesive from previous glue-down, squeaky plywood) that weren't visible at measure. When this happens during install:

- Job pauses mid-install (rooms torn up, furniture displaced)
- Customer is stressed and unprepared for additional cost
- No automation to manage expectations, get subfloor repair approval, and communicate timeline change
- Customer-facing visibility into what's happening is zero

A good snapshot includes a **subfloor surprise workflow**: immediate customer notification, photo sharing capability, approval request, revised timeline, and a re-confirmation of install date after the repair is complete.

### 1.5 The Long Decision Cycle

Large flooring jobs — whole-home hardwood refinishing, commercial LVP installation, tile throughout a new build — have a **4–12 week decision cycle**. Without a long-form nurture sequence, leads go completely cold. Most flooring contractors have nothing between the initial quote email and a single "just checking in" text 6 months later.

**What's needed:**
- 90-day nurture sequence for large uncommitted jobs
- Educational content drips (subfloor prep, floating vs. glue-down, LVP thickness guide)
- Seasonal campaign triggers (spring renovation push, pre-listing flooring refresh)
- Re-engagement campaign for leads dead 90+ days

### 1.6 No-Follow-Up Culture in Flooring

This is the single biggest revenue leak in the industry. Flooring contractors are trade people — they love the craft, but sales follow-up is foreign. Industry data consistently shows:

- **48% of salespeople never follow up after a quote**
- **Only 10% follow up more than 3 times**
- **80% of sales require 5+ follow-up touchpoints**

A flooring snapshot that automates 8–10 follow-up touches across 30 days (with varying channels: SMS, email, voicemail drop) will outperform any competitor relying on manual salespeople to remember to call.

### 1.7 Seasonal Demand Volatility

Flooring has pronounced seasonal patterns:
- **Peak:** Spring (April–June) — renovations, home sales season, spring cleaning motivation
- **Secondary peak:** September–October — pre-winter warm-up, new home purchases closing
- **Slow period:** November–January — budget consolidation, holiday distraction
- **Insurance/water damage:** Year-round, but spikes after spring flooding, pipe freeze events in winter

A snapshot that ignores seasonality leaves campaigns on the table. Seasonal workflows — spring launch, fall refresh, insurance/disaster response — are automatic revenue triggers.

### 1.8 The Referral and Neighbourhood Gold Rush

Every completed flooring job creates neighbour curiosity. "Did you see [client]'s new hardwood? Who did that?" This is where most flooring contractors bleed money. No system captures the referral moment. A post-install referral and neighbour campaign — "We just finished [street area], did any of your neighbours mention their floors?" — turns every job into a lead generation event.

Real estate agent referrals are equally untapped. Pre-sale flooring refreshes (new LVP, carpet stretching, hardwood buff and coat) are high-value, fast-close jobs that agents need done in 7–14 days before listing. No flooring company is systematically nurturing real estate agent relationships at scale.

## SECTION 3: PIPELINE ARCHITECTURE

### 3.1 Pipeline Overview — Four Tracks

The 1app flooring snapshot runs four dedicated pipelines, each with its own stages, workflows, and conversion logic. This is not one generic pipeline — it's four industry-specific tracks that reflect how flooring jobs actually work.

---

### PIPELINE 1: RESIDENTIAL INSTALL (Hardwood / LVP / Laminate / Carpet / Tile)

**Use cases:** New flooring installation in single-family homes, condos, townhomes. All product categories.

**Stages:**

| Stage | Name | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | New Inquiry | Lead comes in (web form, Google LSA, referral, Kijiji). Speed-to-lead fires. |
| 2 | Measure Scheduled | In-home measure booked. Confirmation + reminder sequences fire. |
| 3 | Measure Complete | Estimator has been out. Estimate is being prepared. |
| 4 | Estimate Sent | Quote delivered (email/PDF). Estimate follow-up sequence begins. |
| 5 | Sample Requested | Customer wants to see physical samples before deciding. |
| 6 | Sample Delivered | Samples are with the customer. Sample approval workflow fires. |
| 7 | Sample Approved | Customer has selected product. Ready to close. |
| 8 | Estimate Approved / Deposit Taken | Job is booked. Deposit collected or PO issued. |
| 9 | Material Ordered | Product ordered from supplier. Lead time communicated to customer. |
| 10 | Material Arrived | Product in-shop or ready to bring to site. |
| 11 | Install Scheduled | Install date confirmed with customer. Day-before + morning-of reminders fire. |
| 12 | Install In Progress | Active job. Subfloor discovery workflow on standby. |
| 13 | Install Complete — Review Requested | Job done. Review request + neighbour campaign fires. |
| 14 | Won | Full payment collected. Job closed. Referral automation begins. |
| 15 | Lost / Nurture | Quote not accepted. Enters 90-day nurture sequence. |

### PIPELINE 2: REFINISHING & RESTORATION (Hardwood Sand & Finish, Buff & Coat, Screen & Recoat)

**Use cases:** Existing hardwood floors being refinished, screened and recoated, or buff and coat (no sanding). Different from install — no material lead time, different timelines, customer displacement (must leave during finish cure).

**Stages:**

| Stage | Name | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | New Inquiry | Lead in. Speed-to-lead fires. Floor photo request SMS triggers. |
| 2 | Measure/Assessment Scheduled | Estimator books in-home assessment of existing floor condition. |
| 3 | Assessment Complete | Floor assessed: square footage, wood species, condition, current finish, scratch depth, moisture. Estimate being built. |
| 4 | Estimate Sent | Quote delivered with refinishing options: full sand & finish vs. screen & recoat vs. buff & coat. |
| 5 | Estimate Follow-Up Active | Follow-up sequence running. Education drips on what each service delivers. |
| 6 | Booked / Deposit Taken | Customer committed. Prep instructions sent (furniture removal, pet plan, temp displacement info). |
| 7 | Prep Confirmed | Customer confirms furniture out, space ready. Day-before reminder fires. |
| 8 | Job In Progress | Active sanding/finishing. Customer away from home. |
| 9 | Cure Time Communication | Floors coated. Cure time instructions sent (no foot traffic X hours, no rugs Y days). |
| 10 | Completion Sign-Off | Customer returns, signs off on work. Final payment collected. |
| 11 | Review + Referral | Review request fires. Neighbour campaign triggers. |
| 12 | Won | Closed. |
| 13 | Lost / Nurture | Enters nurture with refinishing education content. |

### PIPELINE 4: INSURANCE / WATER DAMAGE REPLACEMENT

**Use cases:** Water damage from burst pipes, flooding, appliance leaks, basement seepage. Insurance-funded floor replacement. Emergency timeline. Different stakeholders: homeowner + insurance adjuster.

**Stages:**

| Stage | Name | Description |
|---|---|---|

| 2 | Insurance Confirmed | Homeowner confirms claim filed. Insurance company and adjuster details collected. |

| 9 | Material Ordered | Rush order placed. Lead time communicated. |

## SECTION 4: WORKFLOW LIBRARY

### 4.1 Core Automation Workflows (30 Workflows)

---

#### WF-001: Speed-to-Lead (All Pipelines)

**Trigger:** New contact created via web form, Facebook Lead Ad, Google LSA webhook
**Goal:** Contact within 5 minutes of inquiry submission
**Channel sequence:**
1. **Immediate (0 min):** SMS — "Hey [First Name], this is [Contractor Name] from [Company]. You just reached out about flooring — I'd love to get you taken care of. Are you available for a quick call?" [Branch: if response → notify rep. If no response in 5 min → continue]
2. **5 min:** AI voice drop or direct call attempt from rep
3. **15 min:** Email — Introduction + booking link for in-home measure
4. **1 hour:** SMS — "Still here when you're ready. Click here to grab a time for your free in-home measure: [link]"
5. **Same day:** Internal task created for rep to make personal call
6. **Day 1 (evening):** SMS — "Quick note — we offer free in-home measures with zero obligation. What flooring are you thinking about?" [Response tracking]

**Branch logic:** Once contact responds or books → remove from speed-to-lead, enter appropriate pipeline stage. If no response after Day 1 evening message → enter slow-burn nurture.

---

#### WF-002: In-Home Measure Confirmation & Reminder

**Trigger:** Contact moved to "Measure Scheduled" stage
**Goal:** Zero no-shows, customer prepared for the measure visit

1. **Immediate (booking confirmed):** Email — Confirmation with estimator name, date, time window, what to have ready (rooms being measured, inspiration photos if any)
2. **SMS:** "Locked in! Your free flooring measure is set for [Day] at [Time] with [Estimator Name]. We'll call 15 min before arrival. Questions? Text anytime."
3. **Day before:** Email reminder — "See you tomorrow!" with prep tips (clear access to rooms, note anything about subfloor history like previous moisture issues)
4. **Day before PM:** SMS reminder — "Reminder: your flooring measure is tomorrow at [Time]. [Estimator Name] will call 15 min before arrival. Looking forward to it."
5. **Day of (2 hours before):** SMS — "Today's the day! [Estimator Name] is on their way to you around [Time]. They'll text when 15 min out."
6. **Day of (en route):** Manual trigger from rep or automated → SMS — "[Estimator] is ~15 min away!"

**No-show recovery:** If appointment not confirmed within 24 hours of booking → re-confirmation SMS with easy reschedule link.

**Trigger:** Contact moved to "Sample Delivered" stage
**Goal:** Get approval decision or alternative request within 5 days

**Sequence:**

| Day | Channel | Message |
|---|---|---|
| Day of delivery | SMS | "Your [product name] sample has been dropped off (or mailed)! Try it in different rooms and different lighting. What's your gut reaction?" |
| Day 1 | SMS | "How's the [LVP/hardwood/tile] looking in your space? Remember to try it next to your [cabinets/trim/furniture colour] — that's where it really tells the story." |
| Day 2 | Email | Visual guide: "How to evaluate a flooring sample in your home" — lighting tips, photographic side-by-side, what to look for in graining/texture |

- "Not sure / want to see more" → Trigger: Alt Sample Workflow — offer 2-3 alternatives based on product category, repeat sample delivery flow
- "Not for us" → Ask: "What didn't work? Colour? Texture? Price?" → Route to new quote conversation

#### WF-005: Install Day Communication

**Trigger:** Contact moved to "Install Scheduled" stage
**Goal:** Zero surprises on install day, customer confident and prepared

1. **7 days before:** Email — "Your install is coming up! Here's how to prepare:" (furniture removal, clear path for materials, pets, remove baseboards if not doing as part of service, where crew will need to access)
2. **3 days before:** SMS — "3 days until install day! Quick reminder: [specific prep items]. Any questions before the crew arrives?"
3. **Day before:** Email — Full install day guide: time window, crew size, what happens during install, noise/dust expectations, when to expect completion
4. **Day before PM:** SMS — "Tomorrow's the big day! The crew will arrive between [time window]. We'll call 30 min before. See you then!"
5. **Morning of:** SMS — "Good morning [Name]! The team is on their way. Arrival estimate: [Time]. Excited for you to see the transformation!"
6. **During install:** Optional manual trigger → SMS mid-install progress photo share
7. **End of day:** SMS — "The team is wrapping up. [Estimator/PM Name] will do a walkthrough with you shortly. Any concerns before we pack up?"

#### WF-006: Subfloor Discovery Workflow (Flooring-Specific — Unique)

**Trigger:** Rep or installer manually triggers "Subfloor Issue Discovered" tag
**Goal:** Transparent communication, customer approval, no stalled jobs

1. **Immediate:** SMS from rep — "Hi [Name], this is [Name] from [Company]. Our crew just uncovered something we need to discuss before we proceed. Are you available for a quick call in the next 15 minutes?"
2. **If no response in 15 min:** Email — "[Company] Subfloor Update for [Address]" — explains what was found (with photo), options and costs, timeline impact
3. **Email (sent simultaneously):** Customer-facing subfloor issue summary with: description of issue, photos, repair options (A: full repair = cost + timeline, B: limited repair = cost + timeline + limitation note), a "Proceed with Option A/B" reply trigger
4. **Follow-up call from estimator:** Internal task triggered for estimator to call customer within 30 minutes
5. **Approval captured:** When customer approves via reply or phone — system logs approval, moves to "Subfloor Repair Approved" substage, revised install date sent
6. **Post-repair install confirmation:** New install date confirmation triggers Install Day workflow (WF-005) with updated dates

## APPENDIX A: FLOORING INDUSTRY GLOSSARY (FOR SNAPSHOT LANGUAGE REFERENCE)

- **LVP:** Luxury Vinyl Plank — waterproof, floating floor, popular for basements, kitchens, high-traffic areas
- **SPC:** Stone Plastic Composite — rigid core LVP variant, more stable
- **WPC:** Wood Plastic Composite — softer underfoot LVP variant
- **Hardwood:** Solid wood planks, nailed or stapled to subfloor
- **Engineered Hardwood:** Real wood veneer over plywood core — more stable, can float or glue
- **Laminate:** HDF core with photographic wear layer — floating floor, not waterproof
- **AC Rating:** Abrasion Class for laminate (AC1 light residential → AC5 commercial heavy)
- **Wear Layer:** LVP's protective top coat — measured in mil. 6 mil residential, 12+ mil commercial
- **Underlayment:** Foam or cork cushion under floating floors — sound dampening, slight moisture barrier
- **Subfloor:** The structural floor layer beneath the finished floor (plywood, OSB, concrete slab)
- **DPM:** Damp Proof Membrane — vapour barrier for concrete slab installs
- **Floating Floor:** Not attached to subfloor — planks click together and float over it
- **Glue-Down:** Flooring adhered directly to subfloor with adhesive — commercial LVP, solid hardwood on concrete
- **Nail-Down:** Hardwood fastened to wood subfloor with cleats or staples (requires pneumatic nailer)
- **Sand & Finish:** Full hardwood refinishing — sanding to bare wood, new stain, new finish coats
- **Screen & Recoat:** Light scuff of existing finish, new topcoat applied — no stain change
- **Buff & Coat:** Even lighter surface prep — removes surface contamination, applies new finish coat
- **Transition Strip:** Metal or wood profile piece that bridges two floor levels or materials (T-bar, reducer, end cap)
- **Square Footage:** Total area to be covered — calculated length × width, subtract fixed elements (islands, closets)
- **Plank Width:** Flooring board width — narrow (2.25"–3.5"), wide (5"+), extra wide (7"+)
- **Sight Lines:** How flooring runs relative to room geometry and light — usually parallel to longest wall or light source
- **Grout:** Filler material between tile joints — sanded (wide joints), unsanded (narrow joints), epoxy (high moisture)
- **Tile Layout:** How tile is arranged — straight, brick offset, herringbone, chevron, diagonal
- **Subfloor Moisture:** Moisture content in concrete or wood — critical to test before install (moisture meter)
- **Leveling Compound:** Self-leveling product to flatten uneven concrete subfloor
- **Quarter Round / Shoe Moulding:** Trim piece that covers gap between flooring and baseboard
- **Carpet Stretching:** Re-stretching loose or bubbled carpet using power stretcher — cheap but high-value job
- **Carpet Padding:** Foam or fiber pad beneath carpet — affects feel, sound absorption, carpet lifespan
- **FRT:** Face-Reduced Threshold — slim transition for nearly-level floor changes
