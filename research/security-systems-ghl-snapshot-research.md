## Executive Summary

The security systems industry is a high-LTV, recurring-revenue vertical with predictable sales cycles, strong referral networks, and a persistent gap between the quality of service these companies deliver and the sophistication of their marketing and follow-up systems. Most security dealers rely on phone leads, paper forms, and tribal knowledge — zero automation, zero pipeline visibility, and chronic leak of warm leads who never got a second call.

A well-built GHL snapshot for this vertical can be the most impactful technology adoption a security company makes. Speed-to-lead alone — auto-responding within 60 seconds of a web form submit — can increase close rates by 20–40% in a vertical where many dealers call back the next day.

This document covers everything needed to build the highest-converting, most comprehensive GHL security systems snapshot on the market.

## Section 1: Industry Overview & Business Pain Points

### 1.1 Who These Companies Are

Security systems companies range from:
- **Solo operators/technicians** doing residential installs (1–5 trucks)
- **Regional alarm dealers** with monitoring contracts and a small inside sales team
- **Commercial integrators** specializing in access control, CCTV, IP cameras, NVR/DVR systems
- **Smart home integrators** layering security onto broader home automation (Lutron, Control4, Alarm.com)
- **Franchise/dealer networks** under major brands (ADT dealers, Alarm.com dealers, Telus SmartHome dealers in Canada)

The most common business model: **one-time installation revenue + recurring monthly monitoring contract (RMR)**. Monitoring contracts are the lifeblood — a company with 500 monitoring contracts at $35–$65/month has a built-in annuity before a single install happens.

### 1.2 Core Business Pain Points

**Pain Point 1: Long Sales Cycle for Monitoring Contracts**
- Homeowners frequently request a "free security assessment" but stall on signing
- Commercial clients often require 2–3 site visits, proposals, revisions, and internal approval
- Sales reps lose track of where deals sit — no CRM = no follow-up = lost RMR
- **GHL Solution:** Structured pipeline with task automation between stages, proposal follow-up sequences

**Pain Point 2: Equipment Cost Objections**
- "I can get a Ring doorbell for $99 — why do I need a $3,000 system?"
- DIY competition from Ring, SimpliSafe, Google Nest has commoditized entry-level residential
- Dealers need a value story around professional monitoring, ULC listing, central station response, insurance discounts, and integrated systems
- **GHL Solution:** Automated nurture email/SMS sequence with education content addressing DIY objections

**Pain Point 3: Speed-to-Lead Failure**
- Most security companies call back leads from Google/Facebook the next morning
- Research shows 78% of sales go to the company that responds first
- A lead filling out a "free assessment" form at 8pm on Sunday expects a text immediately
- **GHL Solution:** Instant SMS auto-responder + 2-way conversation within 90 seconds

**Pain Point 4: Installation Scheduling & Dispatch Complexity**
- Booking an install requires knowing technician availability, equipment inventory, and job duration
- Rescheduling cascades into multiple customer calls and dispatcher headaches
- **GHL Solution:** Calendar booking flow with service type selection, technician assignment, and automated reminders

**Pain Point 5: Monitoring Contract Renewal & Churn**
- Annual or multi-year contracts expire — companies often don't know until the customer calls to cancel
- No proactive renewal outreach = reactive churn management
- **GHL Solution:** Monitoring renewal workflow triggered 90/60/30 days before contract expiry

**Pain Point 6: Referral Capture**
- Security companies live and die by referral, but most have no system to ask for referrals at the right moment
- The best time to ask: 48–72 hours after a clean install when the customer is impressed
- **GHL Solution:** Post-install review + referral request automation

**Pain Point 7: Insurance Discount Campaign Awareness**
- Most homeowners don't know that a professionally monitored alarm system (ULC listed in Canada / UL listed in US) qualifies for 5–15% home insurance premium discounts
- This objection-flipper is massively underused in marketing
- **GHL Solution:** Dedicated nurture sequence with insurance savings hook, lead magnet PDF

**Pain Point 8: Commercial vs. Residential Differentiation**
- Commercial jobs (access control, IP camera systems, NVR/DVR, alarm panels) require different sales motions, longer timelines, and technical proposals
- Most dealers mix these in the same pipeline and lose commercial leads to larger integrators
- **GHL Solution:** Separate pipeline with commercial-specific stages, proposal tracking, and technical assessment workflow

## Section 5: Lead Sources & Acquisition Strategy

### 5.1 Primary Lead Sources for Security Companies

**Google Local Services Ads (LSA)**
- Highest-intent inbound — customer is actively searching "home security installation near me," "alarm company [city]," "CCTV installation"
- LSA leads arrive via phone call or message — speed-to-lead is everything
- GHL integration: Missed call text back fires within 60 seconds, 2-way SMS conversation opens
- LSA ad categories: Home Security Systems, Alarm Systems, CCTV Installation, Access Control

**Google Search Ads (PPC)**
- Target: "monitored home alarm," "security camera installation," "commercial access control system," "ULC alarm company"
- Funnel: Form → GHL landing page → booking calendar
- GHL integration: Facebook/Google lead ads connect directly to pipelines via webhook

**Facebook/Instagram Ads**
- Insurance discount hook performs very well: "Your alarm could save you $200/year on home insurance"
- Before/after creative: "From zero coverage to fully monitored in one afternoon"
- Lead form ads feed directly into GHL Speed-to-Lead workflow

**Referrals — Homeowners**
- Best source for residential — existing customers referring neighbors
- GHL: Post-install referral request workflow (Section 4.4) + referral tracking tag

**Referrals — Real Estate Agents**
- Agents close homes constantly — new homeowners want security
- GHL: Partner referral loop (Section 4.8) + co-marketing materials
- Pitch: "We make you look good by protecting your clients' new homes within days of closing."

**Referrals — Insurance Brokers**
- Brokers recommend monitored alarms to clients seeking lower premiums
- GHL: Partner referral workflow + automated insurance discount certificate generator

**New Home Builders**
- Pre-wire security during construction = guaranteed install at closing
- Volume: one builder relationship = 50–200+ installs/year
- GHL: Commercial pipeline stage for "Builder Partnership," separate onboarding sequence

**Door Knocking / D2D Canvassing**
- Still widely used in residential security, especially after a neighborhood incident
- GHL integration: Mobile-friendly intake form, rep enters prospect data on-site, Speed-to-Lead sequence begins immediately
- Tag: "door-knock" for tracking ROI by acquisition channel

**Community Events / Home Shows**
- Local home and garden shows, community safety events
- GHL: QR code on booth → landing page → instant opt-in → 7-day nurture

**Social Proof / Google Reviews**
- Reviews drive LSA ranking + conversion rate
- GHL: Post-install review request automation builds review volume systematically

## Section 8: Insurance Premium Reduction Campaign — Full Build Spec

### 8.1 Campaign Overview

**Goal:** Generate residential leads by leading with the insurance savings hook — then convert to security assessment bookings.

**Why this works:**
- Costs money → saving money angle is strong in any economic environment
- Homeowners are already thinking about insurance at renewal time
- Positions the dealer as an advisor, not a salesperson

## Section 14: Compliance & Disclaimer Notes

### 14.1 Insurance Discount Claims

- Claims about insurance discounts should be framed as "up to X%" and qualified with "depending on your insurance provider and policy"
- Recommend clients verify with their own insurance broker
- In Canada: ULC listing is the standard; specify "ULC C329 for burglary" or "ULC C319 for fire" where relevant
- In US: UL listing is the equivalent standard

### 14.2 Monitoring Language

- Avoid claiming police response times — these vary by municipality and dispatching agency
- Say "central station monitoring" not "police monitoring"
- "Priority dispatch to emergency services" is safe; "police response in X minutes" is not

### 14.3 Data Collection

- All form fields comply with CASL (Canada) / CAN-SPAM (US) opt-in requirements
- Include opt-in language on all forms: "By submitting this form, you agree to receive communications from [Company Name]. You can unsubscribe at any time."
- Do not collect financial data, SIN/SSN, or detailed personal security information via GHL forms

## Appendix A: Industry Terminology Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| Alarm panel | Main control unit of a security system; manages zones, monitoring, and siren output |
| Motion sensor (PIR) | Passive infrared sensor detecting heat signature of movement |
| Door/window sensor | Magnetic contact sensor that triggers when opened |
| CCTV | Closed-circuit television — traditional analog camera system |
| IP camera | Network-connected digital camera; higher resolution, remote access |
| NVR | Network Video Recorder — records footage from IP cameras |
| DVR | Digital Video Recorder — records footage from analog CCTV cameras |
| Access control | System managing who can enter/exit specific doors or areas |
| Keypad | User interface for arming/disarming alarm panel |
| Monitoring contract | Agreement for central station to monitor alarm 24/7 for recurring monthly fee |
| RMR | Recurring Monthly Revenue — from monitoring contracts |
| ULC listed | Underwriters Laboratories of Canada certification — required for insurance discounts and commercial-grade systems in Canada |
| UL listed | Underwriters Laboratories (US equivalent of ULC) |
| Central station | 24/7 alarm monitoring facility that responds to alarm signals |
| Smart home integration | Connecting security to smart speakers, door locks, thermostats, lighting |
| Video doorbell | Doorbell with integrated camera and 2-way audio |
| Electric strike | Door locking mechanism controlled electronically |
| Magnetic lock (magloc) | Electromagnetic lock holding door closed; releases on power loss |
| Proximity fob/card | Access control credential using RFID technology |
| PTZ camera | Pan-tilt-zoom camera that can be remotely repositioned |
| License plate reader | Specialized camera that captures and logs vehicle plates |
| Zone | Individual sensor or sensor group in an alarm system |
| Keyholding service | Central station physically attends to property after alarm trigger |
