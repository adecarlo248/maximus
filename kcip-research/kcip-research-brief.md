# KCIP Research Brief — 1APP Meeting Prep

## Bottom line
KCIP is not weak operationally — the public proof says the opposite. The gap is front-office capture: the website and social presence show a real local trades business, but the quote/booking path is clunky, generic, and likely manual. That is where 1APP should position: not “we make websites,” but “we stop good pool/backyard leads from slipping through the cracks while you are on job sites.”

## Public signals reviewed
- Website: https://kcip.ca/
- Public Facebook/profile mirror and search snippets for Kawartha Custom Inground Pools
- Competitor sites/snippets: Kawartha Pool, Hubble Landscaping, Peterborough Pool & Patio, One Landscape, Kawartha Custom Homes Landscaping

## KCIP strengths
- Established since 2017.
- Strong service breadth: inground pools, openings/closings, revamps, liners, leak detection, concrete, turf, putting greens, armour stone, masonry, cabanas/gazebos/custom lighting, sport courts.
- Strong differentiators:
  - Reviving neglected pools others say are too far gone.
  - Custom-shaped pools and tanning ledges.
  - Custom liner measuring and Wrinkle-Free Guarantee.
  - CommunityVotes wins/social proof.
- Owner story and personality are visible on Facebook — useful for trust.

## KCIP gaps / likely pain
- Shopify site is an odd fit for a quote-driven trades company.
- “Free Quote” route appears to behave like a generic service page, not a proper quote intake funnel.
- No obvious live booking calendar for estimates.
- No obvious structured estimate form that collects job type, location, timeline, photos, budget range, urgency.
- No obvious missed-call text-back or AI answering system.
- Follow-up is likely manual: voicemails, Messenger, texts, phone notes, memory.
- No visible pipeline: lead → contacted → estimate booked → quoted → won/lost.

## Suggested workflows
1. Missed-call text back
   - Trigger: missed call during job-site hours.
   - SMS: “Thanks for calling KCIP — are you looking for pool service, a liner/revamp, new pool install, concrete/turf, or a full backyard project?”
   - Goal: capture intent instantly.

2. AI Voice receptionist
   - Answers overflow/after-hours calls.
   - Collects name, phone, location, service type, urgency, timeline, project notes.
   - Routes urgent service/leak issues differently from large design/build estimates.

3. Website quote + estimate booking
   - Replace generic Free Quote with a structured “Book Your Backyard Estimate” path.
   - Intake fields: service category, address/town, preferred timeline, photos, approximate scope, best contact method.
   - Qualified leads can book controlled estimate windows.

4. CRM pipeline
   - New Lead → Contacted → Estimate Booked → Estimate Completed → Quote Sent → Follow-Up → Won/Lost.
   - Scott can see every opportunity without digging through texts and voicemail.

5. Quote follow-up sequence
   - 1 day: thanks + recap.
   - 3 days: answer questions + proof point.
   - 7 days: project photo/testimonial/social proof.
   - 14 days: “Should we keep this open or revisit later?”

6. Seasonal reactivation
   - Spring: pool openings/inspection reminders.
   - Summer: leak detection/service/upgrade prompts.
   - Fall: closings/winterization.
   - Off-season: book 2027 backyard transformations early.

7. Review/referral engine
   - After completed job: SMS/email review request.
   - If positive: ask for Google/Facebook review and referral.
   - If negative: route privately before it becomes public.

## Meeting angle
Lead with the business owner pain, not software:

“You’ve clearly built a real reputation. The issue I see is not your work — it’s that the front office still depends too much on you being available. When you’re on site, driving, quoting, or dealing with a crew issue, a good lead can hit voicemail or a generic form. 1APP gives KCIP an always-on front desk that responds, qualifies, books, and follows up automatically.”
