## The Short Version

Tony DeCarlo is a man in the middle of his most important transformation. He's a builder, a protector, a provider who has lived through real pain and refuses to be defined by it. He's not behind — he's carrying everything he has lived into a new chapter.

---

## His Story

**Roots:** Born and raised just outside Peterborough, Ontario. Middle-class home. Father built a hockey rink in the backyard with his own hands. Love, structure, hard work, and discipline were the air he breathed growing up. Hockey wasn't just a sport — it was discipline, brotherhood, and learning how to show up when things get hard.

**Core wound (age 19):** His father fell into addiction. The business, the home, the marriage — it all fell apart fast. This shattered his certainty that good homes stay good if you work hard enough. It planted fear in his nervous system: *if this can happen to him, it can happen to me too.* It also taught him redemption is real — his father recovered and now helps others through Narcotics Anonymous.

**Career path:** College → police foundations → mental health hospitals → group homes → corrections. He chose these environments because he wanted to help people. But psychologically, he was also drawn to places where others were falling apart — because if he could handle the worst of life, maybe he could control it or understand it. The cost was real: stress, trauma, PTSD, depression, addiction, and significant weight gain.

**His comeback:** Got sober. Lost 65 pounds. Studied fitness and nutrition. Rebuilt himself piece by piece. This is who he is — someone who knows how to come back.

**Now:** Husband. Father. Man in transition. Leaving what is breaking him and building what will free him.

---

## His Psychology (Deep Layer)

**Identity built around usefulness:** Worth = what you can carry, endure, and prove. Hockey reinforced this. He measures himself by performance under pressure, not by quiet self-trust.

**Adaptive to crisis:** He adapted so well to chaos that chaos became his normal. His body eventually said no. He's strong enough to survive chaos, but strong people often get mistaken for people who can *live* in chaos forever.

**Fear of exposure, not effort:** He doesn't fear working hard. He fears that if success gets real, so does the risk of losing it. Part of him still hedges. Perfectionism shows up not as laziness — it's old survival logic dressed as hesitation.

**The internal war:** Two voices. One says he's powerful, capable, chosen, able to build anything. The other — born in chaos and reinforced by burnout — whispers "maybe it's too late, maybe it's too risky." Fear vs. faith. Old identity vs. emerging identity.

**His real problem:** Not capability. Self-trust hasn't caught up to the evidence yet. He's already proven more than enough.
