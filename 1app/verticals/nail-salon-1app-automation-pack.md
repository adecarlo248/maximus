## 1. Industry Snapshot

Nail salons live and die by chair utilization, repeat visits, technician scheduling, and local reputation. Most shops do not have a lead problem as much as they have a leakage problem: missed calls, slow replies, poor rebooking, no-show gaps, inconsistent review requests, and no system for lapsed clients.

### Web research signals used

- Vagaro's nail salon software page highlights the core market needs: 24/7 booking, automatic reminders, walk-in/waitlist management, client preference notes, no-show protection with cards/deposits, and loyalty programs.
- Fresha's nail business positioning reinforces the same category demand: nail salons want online booking, scheduling, client management, payments, marketing, and marketplace visibility.
- Additional vertical assumptions are based on common salon operations: recurring 2-4 week maintenance visits, service-menu complexity, technician/service matching, bridal/group inquiries, gift cards, memberships/packages, reviews, and seasonal promos.

## 2. Nail Salon Pain Points

### Revenue leakage

1. **Missed calls during services**
   - Staff are hands-on with clients and cannot answer every call.
   - Caller intent is usually high: booking, price check, availability, reschedule, walk-in inquiry.
   - If nobody replies quickly, the prospect books somewhere else.

2. **No-shows and last-minute cancellations**
   - Empty chairs create immediate lost revenue.
   - Longer services like acrylic sets, gel extensions, nail art, fills, and pedicure packages are especially costly when abandoned.
   - Many salons do not collect deposits or confirm appointments properly.

3. **Weak rebooking system**
   - Nails are naturally recurring, but many salons leave rebooking to memory.
   - Clients often need fills, gel refreshes, pedicures, or removals every 2-4 weeks.
   - No system means repeat clients drift to competitors.

4. **Inconsistent review generation**
   - Happy clients leave without being asked.
   - Unhappy clients are more likely to post publicly if the salon does not capture feedback privately first.
   - Reviews heavily influence local search and walk-in trust.

5. **Service-menu confusion**
   - Clients ask: gel vs shellac, acrylic vs bio gel, fill vs full set, simple vs complex nail art, removal, repair, pedicure add-ons.
   - Wrong bookings create schedule gaps, rushed technicians, and awkward price conversations.

6. **Birthday, seasonal, and event revenue left unused**
   - Salons can drive easy repeat revenue through birthday promos, holiday nails, wedding season, prom/graduation, Valentine's Day, Mother's Day, summer pedicures, Halloween, Christmas, and New Year's.
   - Most salons post randomly instead of running repeatable campaigns.

7. **Bridal and group bookings are under-qualified**
   - Group bookings need date, headcount, services, timeline, deposit policy, and coordinator contact.
   - Without intake, salons waste time answering back-and-forth messages.

8. **Membership/package opportunities are under-sold**
   - Regular clients are ideal for monthly manicure/pedicure packages, VIP perks, prepaid bundles, and loyalty offers.
   - Many salons rely on one-off appointments instead of locking in predictable visits.

9. **Staff/front desk overload**
   - Reception has to answer pricing questions, explain services, confirm appointments, manage no-shows, handle DMs, and chase reviews.
   - Automation should remove repetitive work without sounding robotic.

## 5. Recommended Pipelines and Stages

### Pipeline 1: Nail Booking Leads

1. **New Inquiry**
   - Entry: missed call, form, chat, SMS, social lead, website booking question
   - Exit: AI/staff has replied or booking link sent

2. **Service Qualified**
   - Entry: desired service captured
   - Exit: preferred date/time and client info captured

3. **Booking Link Sent**
   - Entry: AI/staff sent booking link or proposed times
   - Exit: appointment booked or follow-up required

4. **Appointment Booked**
   - Entry: appointment confirmed on calendar
   - Exit: appointment completed, cancelled, or no-show

5. **Needs Staff Review**
   - Entry: complex nail art, group booking, pricing dispute, complaint, policy exception, client asks for human
   - Exit: staff resolves and moves contact forward

6. **No-Show / Cancelled**
   - Entry: missed appointment or cancellation
   - Exit: rescheduled, closed, or winback later

7. **Completed Visit**
   - Entry: appointment completed
   - Exit: review/rebooking flow started

8. **Won / Repeat Client**
   - Entry: completed appointment and rebooked, joined VIP, bought package, or returned

9. **Lost / Not Interested**
   - Entry: client declines, unreachable after sequence, out of area, wrong fit

### Pipeline 2: Bridal / Group Bookings

1. **New Group Inquiry**
2. **Details Captured**
3. **Staff Review Needed**
4. **Quote / Availability Sent**
5. **Deposit Pending**
6. **Group Booking Confirmed**
7. **Completed**
8. **Lost / Unavailable**

### Pipeline 3: Membership / Package Sales

1. **Eligible Client**
2. **Offer Sent**
3. **Interested**
4. **Follow-Up Needed**
5. **Joined VIP / Purchased Package**
6. **Declined**
7. **Renewal / Repurchase Due**

## 8. Calendars

### Recommended calendar structure

1. **New Client Nail Appointment**
   - For first-time clients needing standard manicure/pedicure services.

2. **Manicure / Gel Manicure**
   - Regular manicure, gel/shellac, basic nail art.

3. **Acrylic / Gel Extensions / Builder Gel**
   - Longer appointments, route to qualified techs only.

4. **Fills / Maintenance**
   - Acrylic fill, builder gel fill, gel refresh.

5. **Pedicure Services**
   - Regular/spa/gel pedicure.

6. **Removal / Repair**
   - Shorter services, may combine with new set if allowed.

7. **Nail Art / Complex Design Request**
   - If complex, send to staff review before confirming.

8. **Bridal / Group Consultation**
   - Intake call or consultation, not the final group appointment.

9. **VIP / Package Member Booking**
   - Optional if salon wants priority calendar or member-only booking.

### Calendar rules

- Add buffer time for complex nail art and removals.
- Require confirmation for new clients.
- Consider deposits for long services or group bookings.
- Auto-escalate if client wants a service not listed.
- Route specific services only to technicians who perform them.

## 10. Workflow AI Builder Prompts

Paste these into GHL AI Builder and then manually review all triggers, wait steps, field names, and message compliance before launch.

### 1. Missed Call Text Back

Build a workflow called "Nail Salon - Missed Call Text Back". Trigger when a call is missed from a new or existing contact. Add tag source_missed_call and nail_new_lead. Immediately send SMS: "Hi {{contact.first_name}}, sorry we missed your call — this is {{business.name}}. Were you trying to book a nail appointment, ask about pricing, or reschedule? Reply here and we’ll help." Wait 2 minutes. If no reply, send SMS: "You can also book here: {{calendar.booking_link}}. If you want nail art, removal, or a specific tech, reply with what you need and we’ll guide you." If contact replies, move to pipeline Nail Booking Leads stage New Inquiry and assign to Conversation AI. If message contains complaint, refund, infection, allergic reaction, angry language, or asks for owner/manager, add tag needs_human and notify staff immediately.

### 2. New Booking Lead Capture

Build a workflow called "Nail Salon - New Booking Lead Capture". Trigger when a form, chat, SMS, or website inquiry is received. Add tag nail_new_lead. Create/update opportunity in Nail Booking Leads pipeline at New Inquiry. Send SMS: "Hi {{contact.first_name}}, thanks for reaching out to {{business.name}}. What service are you looking for — manicure, gel, acrylic/full set, fill, pedicure, removal, repair, or nail art?" Wait for reply. Ask for preferred date/time and whether they want a specific technician. Once service, date/time, name, phone, and email are captured, move opportunity to Service Qualified and send booking link {{calendar.booking_link}} or notify staff if complex_service is detected.

### 3. Booking Link Sent Follow-Up

Build a workflow called "Nail Salon - Booking Link Sent Follow-Up". Trigger when opportunity moves to Booking Link Sent. Wait 3 hours. If no appointment is booked, send SMS: "Quick reminder, {{contact.first_name}} — here’s the booking link for {{business.name}}: {{calendar.booking_link}}. If you’re not sure what to choose, reply with a photo or describe what you want." Wait 24 hours. If still no appointment, send SMS: "Want us to help pick the right service/time? Reply with 1) service you want, 2) preferred day, 3) morning/afternoon/evening." After 3 days with no response, move opportunity to Lost / Not Interested and tag booking_link_no_response.

### 4. Appointment Confirmation

Build a workflow called "Nail Salon - Appointment Confirmation". Trigger when an appointment is booked. Add tag nail_booked. Move opportunity to Appointment Booked. Send SMS immediately: "You’re booked with {{business.name}} for {{appointment.service}} on {{appointment.date}} at {{appointment.time}}. Address: {{business.address}}. Reply C to confirm or R to reschedule. Policy: {{custom.cancellation_policy}}" If deposit is required, send deposit instructions and tag deposit_required. If client replies R, send reschedule link and notify staff if needed.

### 5. 48-Hour and 24-Hour Appointment Reminders

Build a workflow called "Nail Salon - Appointment Reminder Sequence". Trigger when appointment is booked. Wait until 48 hours before appointment and send SMS: "Reminder: your {{appointment.service}} appointment at {{business.name}} is {{appointment.date}} at {{appointment.time}}. Reply C to confirm or R to reschedule." Wait until 24 hours before appointment and send SMS: "See you tomorrow at {{appointment.time}}! If you need removal or nail art added, reply now so we can allow enough time." Wait until 3 hours before appointment and send SMS: "Today’s the day — see you at {{business.name}} at {{appointment.time}}. Address: {{business.address}}." If client replies cancel, reschedule, cannot make it, or late, notify staff and move opportunity to Needs Staff Review.

### 6. No-Show Recovery

Build a workflow called "Nail Salon - No-Show Recovery". Trigger when appointment status changes to No Show. Add tags nail_no_show and needs_human if no-show count is greater than 1. Move opportunity to No-Show / Cancelled. Send SMS after 15 minutes: "Hi {{contact.first_name}}, we missed you for your appointment at {{business.name}} today. Want to reschedule? Here’s the link: {{calendar.booking_link}}" Wait 24 hours. If no booking, send SMS: "We know things come up. If you still want your nails done this week, reply with your best day/time and we’ll try to fit you in." If second no-show, notify staff to review deposit requirement before future booking.

### 7. Cancellation Save and Reschedule

Build a workflow called "Nail Salon - Cancellation Save and Reschedule". Trigger when appointment status changes to Cancelled or client message contains cancel/reschedule. Send SMS: "No problem, {{contact.first_name}}. Want to grab another spot before the week fills up? Book here: {{calendar.booking_link}}" Wait 1 day. If not rebooked, send SMS: "Just checking — would you like us to help find a new time for your {{contact.preferred_service}}? Reply with morning, afternoon, or evening." Move to No-Show / Cancelled until rebooked.

### 8. Post-Appointment Review Request

Build a workflow called "Nail Salon - Post Appointment Review Request". Trigger when appointment status changes to Completed. Add tag nail_completed. Wait 1 hour. Send SMS: "Thanks for visiting {{business.name}}, {{contact.first_name}}! How did we do today? Reply 1-5." If reply is 5 or positive sentiment, send: "Love that — it would mean a lot if you left us a quick Google review: {{business.review_link}}" Tag review_request_sent. If reply is 1-3 or negative sentiment, send: "I’m sorry we missed the mark. I’m sending this to our manager so we can make it right." Add tag complaint and needs_human, notify staff immediately, and do not send review link.

### 9. Rebooking at 2 Weeks

Build a workflow called "Nail Salon - 2 Week Rebooking Reminder". Trigger when appointment is completed and last service is acrylic fill, builder gel fill, gel manicure, or service tagged campaign_rebook_2_week. Wait 14 days. Send SMS: "Hi {{contact.first_name}}, it’s about time to freshen up your nails. Want to rebook at {{business.name}} this week? {{calendar.booking_link}}" Wait 3 days. ## 11. AI Voice Setup

### AI Voice welcome message

"Hi, thanks for calling {{business.name}}. I can help you book an appointment, answer basic service questions, help with rescheduling, or take a message for the team. How can I help today?"

### Full AI Voice prompt

You are the AI receptionist for {{business.name}}, a nail salon located at {{business.address}}. Your job is to answer calls warmly, capture booking details, route clients to the correct service, and escalate anything sensitive to salon staff.

Your tone: friendly, calm, polished, concise, and human. Speak like a helpful salon receptionist, not a sales script. Keep answers short. Ask one question at a time.

Primary goals:

1. Help new and returning clients book nail appointments.
2. Capture name, phone, email, service requested, preferred date/time, and technician preference.
3. Identify whether the client needs removal, repair, nail art, or a complex design.
4. Explain basic service categories using the salon-approved service menu.
5. Send or offer the booking link: {{calendar.booking_link}}.
6. Route complex services, group bookings, complaints, pricing exceptions, deposits, and policy issues to staff.
7. Never make promises the salon has not approved.

Business details:

- Salon name: {{business.name}}
- Address: {{business.address}}
- Phone: {{business.phone}}
- Website: {{business.website}}
- Hours: {{business.hours}}
- Booking link: {{calendar.booking_link}}
- Cancellation policy: {{custom.cancellation_policy}}
- No-show policy: {{custom.no_show_policy}}
- Deposit policy: {{custom.deposit_policy}}

Booking intake flow:

1. Ask if they are a new or returning client.
2. Ask what service they want.
3. If they say manicure, ask regular or gel/shellac.
4. If they say acrylic, gel extensions, builder gel, dip, full set, or fill, confirm which one.
5. Ask if they need removal or repair.
6. Ask if they want nail art or a specific design.
7. If nail art is requested, ask whether it is simple, medium, or complex and whether they have an inspiration photo.
8. Ask preferred date and time window.
9. Ask whether they prefer a specific technician.
10. Capture full name, mobile number, and email.
11. If the request is standard, direct them to booking link or offer to have staff confirm availability.
12. If the request is complex, tell them staff will review and follow up.

Service routing rules:

- Standard manicure, gel manicure, regular pedicure, gel pedicure: can route to online booking.
- Acrylic full set, acrylic fill, builder gel, extensions, dip powder: route to appropriate calendar or staff if technician-specific.
- Nail art: if simple, continue booking; if medium/complex, ask for photo and escalate.
- Removal/repair: ask whether they also want a new set or just removal/repair.
- Bridal/group bookings: do not confirm. Capture event date, group size, services, timeline, and coordinator contact, then escalate.
- Same-day appointment requests: capture request and check booking link; if urgent or unclear, escalate.

Escalation triggers:

- Client asks for manager/owner/human.
- Client is upset, complains, mentions injury, infection, allergic reaction, damage, refund, chargeback, legal issue, or bad review.
- Client wants a discount not listed.
- Client disputes price, deposit, cancellation fee, or no-show policy.
- Client requests complex nail art, bridal/group booking, off-hours booking, or a technician not available.
- Client asks medical advice about nails, skin, infection, allergies, or pain.
- Caller is abusive, threatening, or unsafe.

Escalation response:

"I want to make sure the right person helps with that. I’ll pass this to the salon team now so they can follow up directly. What’s the best phone number to reach you?"

Do not say:

- Do not guarantee exact prices unless the service menu has approved price ranges.
- Do not guarantee availability.
- Do not confirm group bookings without staff.
- Do not diagnose nail/skin/health issues.
- Do not promise refunds, free services, or discounts.
- Do not argue about policies.
- Do not say you are human.
- Do not mention internal automations, workflows, CRM, GHL, or 1APP.
- Do not collect credit card numbers over the call.
- Do not continue if the caller asks for a human; escalate.

If asked whether you are AI:

"I’m the salon’s virtual receptionist, here to help with booking and basic questions. If you need the team directly, I can pass your message along."

Call closing:

- If booking link sent: "Perfect — I’ll send the booking link now. If you’re unsure which service to choose, reply to the text and the team can help."
- If staff follow-up needed: "Thanks, I’ve got that noted. The team will review and follow up as soon as they can."
- If appointment confirmed by system: "You’re all set. You’ll receive a confirmation and reminders before your appointment."

## 12. Conversation AI Prompt

You are the SMS/chat assistant for {{business.name}}, a nail salon. Your job is to convert inquiries into booked appointments, answer basic service questions, reduce no-shows, encourage rebooking, support review requests, and escalate sensitive issues to staff.

Tone:

- Friendly, brief, helpful, polished.
- Use natural texting language.
- Ask one question at a time.
- Do not over-explain.
- Do not be pushy.
- Never sound like a generic bot.

Main objectives:

1. Respond quickly to missed calls and new inquiries.
2. Identify the client’s desired service.
3. Capture name, phone, email, preferred date/time, technician preference, removal needs, nail art needs, and new/returning client status.
4. Send the correct booking link: {{calendar.booking_link}}.
5. Encourage rebooking after completed visits.
6. Ask happy clients for reviews using {{business.review_link}}.
7. Route complex or sensitive issues to staff.

Approved booking questions:

- "Are you a new or returning client?"
- "What service are you looking for?"
- "Do you need removal or a repair too?"
- "Are you wanting nail art or a specific design?"
- "Do you have a preferred day/time?"
- "Do you prefer a specific technician?"
- "Can I grab your email for the booking?"
- "Is this for a special event or group booking?"

Service menu routing:

- Pedicure: ask regular, spa, or gel.

- Do not diagnose health/nail/skin issues.
- Do not promise refunds, discounts, or free fixes.

Review logic:

- First ask: "How did we do today? Reply 1-5."
- If 5 or clearly happy: send {{business.review_link}}.
- If 1-3 or negative: apologize briefly, escalate to staff, do not send review link.

Rebooking logic:

- For gel manicure/fills: suggest 2-3 weeks.
- For pedicures/full refresh: suggest 3-4 weeks.
- Message: "Want to lock in your next visit before the good times fill up? {{calendar.booking_link}}"
