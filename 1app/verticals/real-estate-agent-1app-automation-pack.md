## 1. Research Summary & Market Reality

### What matters in this industry
- Real estate is a speed-to-lead business. Online buyer/seller inquiries decay fast; a Harvard Business Review article on online leads noted companies often respond too slowly and that fast response materially improves contact rates.
- Agents live with fragmented lead sources: website forms, Facebook/Instagram ads, REALTOR.ca/IDX inquiries, Zillow-style portals, open house sheets, signs, referrals, missed calls, email, text, and DMs.
- Agents often lose opportunities because they are showing homes, driving, negotiating, or with clients when new leads come in.
- Canadian buyers still commonly finance purchases. NAR generational research notes most buyers finance, and younger buyers often face down payment pressure, debt, and affordability constraints. That makes pre-approval reminders and lender handoff automation useful.
- Canadian real estate marketing must respect CASL. Commercial electronic messages generally require consent, sender identification, and unsubscribe handling.
- Ontario agents operate under TRESA and RECO consumer-protection rules. Automation must avoid misleading claims, unauthorized brokerage/agent representation, legal advice, mortgage advice, price guarantees, or promises of market outcomes.
- CREA controls REALTOR®, REALTORS®, MLS®, and related marks in Canada. Use only when the agent/brokerage is authorized and follow brokerage/board brand rules.

### Sources checked
- RECO — TRESA explained: TRESA is Ontario consumer-protection legislation governing agents and brokerages.
- Government of Canada / ISED — CASL: commercial electronic messages include email/text and focus on consent, identification, and spam prevention.
- CRTC CASL resources: consent, contact lists, unsubscribe, and compliance tips.
- CREA housing market stats: CREA compiles monthly MLS® market activity and price statistics for Canadian markets.
- NAR buyer/seller generational trends: buyers commonly finance purchases; affordability and down payment pressure are major factors.
- HBR — The Short Life of Online Sales Leads: speed-to-lead is a core sales performance issue.

## 2. Ideal Client Profile for 1APP

### Best-fit agents
- Solo agents doing 12+ deals/year who are losing leads due to slow follow-up.
- Small teams with 2–10 agents who need standardized follow-up.
- Listing-heavy agents running open houses and seller valuation ads.
- Buyer agents spending heavily on paid leads and portals.
- Brokerage teams that need appointment booking, recruiting, and review/referral systems.

### Poor-fit agents
- Brand-new agents with no lead flow and no ad budget.
- Agents unwilling to respond to hot lead alerts quickly.
- Agents whose brokerage forbids third-party CRM/automation use.
- Agents who want automation to make regulated claims or replace licensed advice.

## 3. Core Pain Points

1. **Missed calls become lost deals** — a buyer calls from a sign, listing, or ad; agent is unavailable; buyer contacts another agent.
2. **Slow online lead response** — web and ad leads go cold within minutes/hours.
3. **No consistent follow-up** — agent follows up once, then forgets.
4. **Open house leads are wasted** — paper sign-in sheets or spreadsheet leads never get nurtured.
5. **Buyer leads are unqualified** — agent does not know timeline, budget, location, pre-approval, or motivation.
6. **Seller leads require trust-building** — valuation leads are often early-stage and need long-term nurture.
7. **Showings are scheduling-heavy** — back-and-forth messages burn agent time.
8. **Pre-approval gaps stall buyers** — buyers want homes before financing is clear.
9. **Review/referral opportunities are missed** — closed clients would refer, but no system asks.
10. **Referral partner relationships go stale** — mortgage brokers, lawyers, movers, contractors, and inspectors need nurture.
11. **Team recruiting is inconsistent** — agents looking to grow a team do not follow up with potential recruits.
12. **Compliance risk** — careless automation can imply guarantees, agency relationships, or advice that should come from a licensed professional.

## 5. Compliance Guardrails — Ontario/Canada

### Required rules
- All consumer-facing copy must identify the brokerage/agent correctly as approved by the brokerage.
- Do not imply a client relationship or representation agreement before one exists.
- Do not provide legal, mortgage, tax, investment, or appraisal advice.
- Do not guarantee sale price, sale timeline, bidding outcome, financing approval, or investment return.
- Respect CASL: capture consent where required, identify the sender, include unsubscribe language for marketing sequences, and suppress opt-outs.
- For SMS, always support STOP/UNSUBSCRIBE behavior.
- Use REALTOR® / MLS® marks only if authorized and in compliance with CREA/brokerage/board rules.
- Avoid protected-class filtering or discriminatory housing language.
- If a lead asks about agency/representation, commissions, offers, forms, contracts, deposits, financing, or legal risk, escalate to the licensed agent.

### Do-not-say rules for AI
- Do not say “I am your real estate agent.”
- Do not say “This home is a guaranteed good investment.”
- Do not say “You will qualify for a mortgage.”
- Do not say “The seller will accept X.”
- Do not say “You do not need a lawyer.”
- Do not say “This property has no issues” unless confirmed by the agent and documentation.
- Do not advise someone to waive inspections, financing, legal review, or conditions.
- Do not discuss protected characteristics of neighbourhoods, schools, residents, ethnicity, religion, family status, disability, or any discriminatory preference.
- Do not create urgency with fake competing offers.
- Do not use pressure language like “you must act today” unless the agent has confirmed a real deadline.

## 11. Workflow AI Builder Prompts — Paste-Ready

### 1) Missed Call Text Back — Real Estate
```text
Build a workflow called "Real Estate - Missed Call Text Back".
Trigger: Customer Replied or Inbound Call status is missed.
If the contact has tag do_not_contact or opted_out, stop.
Send SMS immediately: "Hi {{contact.first_name}}, sorry I missed your call — this is {{custom_values.agent_name}} with {{custom_values.brokerage_name}}. Were you calling about buying, selling, a specific property, or something else? Reply here and I’ll help point you in the right direction. Reply STOP to opt out."
Wait 3 minutes.
If no reply, send SMS: "Quick check — if this was about a property, send me the address or link and I’ll take a look. If you’d rather book a quick call, here’s my calendar: {{custom_values.buyer_consult_calendar_link}}"
Wait 15 minutes.
If no reply, create task for assigned agent: "Call missed real estate lead within 30 minutes."
Add tag needs_agent_call.
Notify assigned user by SMS and email with contact name, phone, and source.
If contact replies at any point, remove tag needs_agent_call and notify assigned user.
```

## 17. Escalation Rules Matrix

| Situation | AI action | Human action |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer asks to make offer | Stop advice, escalate | Agent calls immediately |
| Seller asks for listing price | Stop advice, book consult/escalate | Agent prepares valuation/CMA |
| Caller asks legal/contract question | Stop advice, escalate | Agent/lawyer handles |
| Mortgage approval question | Refer to mortgage professional, escalate | Agent makes consented intro |
| Existing agent representation | Acknowledge, avoid interference, escalate | Agent reviews appropriate next step |
| Showing request | Qualify, do not confirm unless approved | Agent confirms access/time |
| Angry complaint | Calm acknowledgement, escalate | Agent/brokerage handles |
| Neighbourhood demographic/protected-class question | Refuse politely, redirect to objective resources | Agent handles carefully |
| Opt-out request | Confirm opt-out, suppress | No marketing contact |
| Hot buying/selling timeline | Tag hot_lead, alert | Agent calls fast |
