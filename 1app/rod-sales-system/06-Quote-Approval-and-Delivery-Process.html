1APP Quote Approval & Delivery Process

Purpose

Make quotes fast without letting sales promise scope Tony cannot deliver profitably.

Roles

  • Rod / associate: discovery, draft solution, CRM record, quote-builder draft, follow-up, onboarding completeness.
  • Tony: feasibility, dependencies, delivery estimate, security/technical risk, setup effort, build acceptance.
  • Justin: pricing, discount, payment terms, commercial scope, final quote approval.
  • Client: accurate requirements, written acceptance, timely access/content, approvals, testing, paid-continuation decision.

Quote stages

1. Discovery complete

Required:

  • Problem statement.
  • Current process.
  • Business impact.
  • Relevant baseline with source/date.
  • Financial mechanism and transparent value scenario.
  • Primary success measure, assumptions, and variables outside 1APP's control.
  • Decision-maker.
  • Relevant systems.
  • Desired outcome and deadline.

2. Draft quote created

Rod selects line items or a recommended bundle in the quote builder. Each item must contain scope, dependencies, delivery estimate, setup fee, monthly fee, and usage note. The draft must also explain why the recommended automation maps to the diagnosed problem, how it may create value, and how that value will be measured without making a guarantee.

Rod must also select one offer path:

  • Revenue Reactivation Sprint: approved dormant cohort, 14-day outbound window, written lawful-contact attestation, success event, job-value tier, fee basis/rate/cap, attribution window, campaign ID, outcome owner, and reconciliation method.
  • Paid Implementation Required: advanced, custom, integration-heavy, migration, website, multi-location, complex-routing, or full community-platform work. The paid setup scope and delivery schedule must be approved before work begins.

3. Technical review

Tony chooses one:

  • Approved standard: catalogue scope/timeframe is valid.
  • Approved Sprint: cohort, compliance, tracking, economics, capacity, and launch controls are complete.
  • Approved with changes: adjust scope, price, time, or dependencies.
  • Discovery required: unknown integration/custom request needs a separate technical session.
  • Declined: unacceptable risk or unsupported request.

4. Commercial review

Justin confirms:

  • Total setup and monthly price.
  • Bundle/discount validity.
  • Payment terms and quote expiry.
  • Sprint success event, fee basis/rate/cap, attribution window, and expansion path.
  • Sprint capacity, message-usage treatment, compliance status, and work excluded from the performance offer.
  • No unauthorized guarantee or claim.

5. Quote issued

Quote ID format: 1APP-YYYYMMDD-CLIENT-V#.

Quote must include:

  • Client's current problem and desired outcome.
  • Current baseline, value mechanism, primary success measure, assumptions, and attribution limits.
  • Selected automations and deliverables.
  • Four commercial price categories shown separately: one-time setup, monthly recurring service, usage/pass-through charges, and optional/custom work.
  • HST or other applicable tax shown separately from the four commercial price categories.
  • Estimated delivery measured from Ready-to-Build.
  • 14-day Sprint outbound terms, success event, approved cohort, attribution, reconciliation, and performance-fee cap.
  • A statement that expanded automation, integrations, migrations, AI voice, and custom work are quoted separately.
  • Client responsibilities and secure access requirements.
  • Exclusions, assumptions, support, change-control rule, and expiry date.
  • Acceptance action.

6. Quote accepted and onboarding

  • Record written acceptance.
  • Lock the quote version.
  • Complete the onboarding/handoff template.
  • Collect access through the secure process.
  • Do not start the build until Tony accepts the handoff.

7. Ready-to-Build

Ready-to-Build occurs only when:

  • Quote is accepted.
  • Handoff is complete.
  • Required access/content exists.
  • Technical unknowns are resolved.
  • Client owner and approver are named.
  • Tony accepts the job into the queue.

The delivery estimate starts on this date.

For a Sprint, the 14-day outbound window starts only after Tony confirms that the approved cohort, lawful-contact attestation, suppression handling, messages, tracking, and test pass are complete. Accepting the quote, joining the queue, or uploading a list does not start the window.

8. Scope change

Anything outside the accepted quote becomes:

  • A swap of equal effort approved in writing; or
  • A priced change request with time impact; or
  • A future phase/backlog item.

Sales may not use “small change” to bypass pricing or QA.

Quote follow-up cadence

  • Same day: send quote and short summary.
  • 48 hours: ask what prevents approval; answer questions.
  • Day 5: manual call task.
  • Day 10: confirm priority and decision date.
  • Day 14: close, revise, nurture, or mark lost with reason.

Stop automated follow-up on reply, acceptance, decline, opt-out, or owner takeover.

Metrics

The formal Day-45 performance review uses the definitions, evidence requirements, and action-plan procedure in 09-Day-45-Review-and-Quote-Builder-Practical.md.

  • Average time: audit complete → draft quote.
  • Average time: draft → technical/commercial approval.
  • Quote acceptance rate.
  • Average setup fee and MRR.
  • Discount rate.
  • Handoff rejection rate.
  • Ready-to-Build → launch time.
  • Sprint-to-paid-expansion rate.
  • Percentage of quotes with a verified baseline and primary success measure.
  • Actual result versus the approved value hypothesis for Sprints and completed implementations.
  • 30/60/90-day retention by automation/bundle.
