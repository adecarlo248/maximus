1APP Revenue Reactivation Sprint — Rod's Playbook

Status: Owner review required before client use. Pricing, caps, and compliance language must be approved by Tony and Justin before Rod quotes or signs a Sprint Agreement.

What This Is (say this to a business owner)

"You already paid to get those old leads and past customers sitting in your files. Most of them never bought, or bought once and never came back. We use AI-powered texting to reach back out and try to turn them into qualified estimate opportunities. There is no upfront setup fee. Messaging usage is disclosed before launch, and you pay only when a qualified appointment is kept and your business issues a documented written estimate. A booking or no-show does not count."

This replaces the generic 14-day starter-automation trial. The Sprint is a performance-based engagement built around a business's existing dormant contact list. A business without a usable dormant list can still buy a scoped implementation, but it is not offered a generic free trial.

Where This Idea Comes From — and Where We Changed It

[likely] The mechanics researched below come from Dan Wardrope's The Instant AI Agency material (a paid guide on AI-powered micro-agencies), cross-checked against a third-party review and reader excerpts. Treat the specifics below as directional, not verified financial results:

  • The book's core method: approach a business, ask if they have old/dormant leads, offer to turn them into sales using AI + SMS ("Sales Android"), and propose a backend profit share — up to 50% of the resulting sale.
  • Partner-fit criteria used in the book: sells a product/service with high margin, average sale over $1,000 (unless high-volume e-commerce), already advertising and generating fresh leads, has a database of 1,000+ contacts, and closes at a 30%+ rate.
  • The pitch line used: "Would you prefer 100% of nothing, or 50% of $100k?"

Tony's decision, and why: a raw 20–50% fee applied to total contract value breaks down on large jobs. A $200,000 renovation at 50% is a $100,000 fee—likely more than the contractor's entire profit. Until estimate-to-payment tracking is live, 1APP uses fixed cost-per-verified-estimate pricing. That gives both sides a result they can independently document without trusting self-reported revenue. Revenue-share pricing remains a future connected option, not something Rod may quote now.

Eligibility Checklist — Ask Before You Pitch

A business is a good Sprint fit if most of these are true:

  • [ ] Sells a product or service with healthy margin (rough guide: 25%+ gross margin)
  • [ ] Has at least 250 dormant contacts (old leads and/or past customers who haven't bought or returned in 90+ days); 1,000+ is ideal but not required
  • [ ] Can realistically run and close an appointment once it's booked (existing sales process, even informal)
  • [ ] The dormant list is contactable — real names, real phone numbers, not a stale export with no way to verify who's on it
  • [ ] Willing to confirm real outcomes (appointment kept, job signed, payment collected) so 1APP can invoice accurately
  • [ ] Willing to attest to lawful basis to contact these people (see Compliance, below)

Do not pitch the Sprint to: businesses with no dormant list, businesses in regulated advice/financial/legal/medical/insurance categories without a full separate compliance review, or businesses unwilling to confirm outcomes for billing.

Current Fee Model — Cost Per Verified Estimate

This is the only default Sprint pricing model until 1APP's estimate-to-payment tracking is live and tested. Rod does not quote revenue share, gross-profit share, or a percentage of a sale during this temporary phase.

The client pays a fixed fee only when all three conditions are true:

  1. The Sprint contact keeps the agreed qualified appointment.
  2. The contact meets the written qualification rules agreed before launch.
  3. The business issues a written estimate containing the customer reference, date, and estimate amount—normally within 48 hours of the appointment.

A booking by itself is not billable. A no-show, cancellation, duplicate, active pre-Sprint opportunity, unqualified contact, verbal price, or estimate without evidence is not billable.

Economic Check

Use the client's real historical inputs where available:

Expected gross profit per estimate = average collected job value × gross margin × estimate-to-sale close rate

Recommended 1APP fee = expected gross profit per estimate × 20%

Round the result to the nearest $25 and never exceed the standard band above. If the client cannot provide credible margin and close-rate data, use 35% gross margin and 30% estimate-to-sale as provisional assumptions, label them as assumptions, and require owner approval.

Example: average collected job is $10,000, gross margin is 35%, and 30% of estimates close. Expected gross profit per estimate is $1,050. Twenty percent is $210, rounded to $200 per verified estimate.

Campaign Ceiling

The quote may include a maximum total Sprint fee so the client knows its worst-case exposure. Once the ceiling is reached, 1APP pauses additional billable estimates until the client approves an extension in writing. The ceiling does not turn unqualified or undocumented estimates into billable results.

Fee Model Decision Tree (for Rod, in the moment)

  1. Ask: "What is the average amount you actually collect on a completed job from an estimate?"
  2. Ask for the typical gross margin and historical estimate-to-sale close rate.
  3. Enter those figures into the quote builder. Use its recommended fee unless Tony and Justin approve a different amount.
  4. Read the verified-estimate definition back to the client and agree on the evidence source.
  5. Set a weekly reconciliation day and name the client-side person who approves the estimate list.
  6. If the average job is under $1,000, over $25,000, or the economics are unclear, stop and get owner approval before quoting.
  7. Never imply that a booked appointment alone is billable unless an owner-approved exception is written into the quote.

Attribution and Billing Rules — Non-Negotiable

  • Every contacted person is tagged with a unique Sprint campaign ID before the first message goes out.
  • A contact only counts if: they were on the approved dormant list, they had no active open opportunity or current service agreement with the business in the 90 days before the Sprint, and the booked/won outcome is logged with a timestamp.
  • During the temporary cost-per-estimate phase, the fee is billed only after a verified estimate meets the written definition and appears in the weekly reconciliation ledger.
  • A later sale, refund, cancellation, or loss does not change a properly earned estimate fee because 1APP is being paid for producing the verified estimate opportunity—not guaranteeing the client's close.
  • Duplicate estimates for the same contact and substantially the same project count once unless a new appointment and materially different scope are documented.
  • Every one of these rules goes into the written Sprint Agreement before any message is sent. Rod never runs a Sprint on a verbal handshake.

Compliance — Read Before Every Pitch

"Old leads" does not automatically mean 1APP has permission to text them. In Canada, commercial text messages are Canadian Anti-Spam Legislation (CASL) commercial electronic messages. [likely] CASL generally requires either express consent, or a limited implied-consent window (commonly cited as up to 2 years for an existing business relationship, or about 6 months following an inquiry) — verify current CASL guidance before relying on these windows, since this summary is not legal advice. Every message must clearly identify the business sending it and include a working way to opt out.

Rod's job is not to make this determination himself. Rod's job is to:

  1. Tell the business owner, plainly, that they are responsible for confirming they have a lawful basis to contact each person on the list.
  2. Get that confirmation in writing as part of the Sprint Agreement (a checkbox/attestation, not a verbal "yeah it's fine").
  3. Make sure the messaging itself always identifies the business and always includes an opt-out.
  4. Immediately stop messaging any contact who replies STOP, asks not to be contacted, or is on a suppression list.
  5. Escalate to Tony before running a Sprint for any business in a regulated category (insurance, financial services, legal, medical/dental, mortgage) — these need a dedicated compliance review before Rod quotes anything.

Objection Handling

  • "Why would I pay before the job closes?" → "You are not paying for a calendar booking. You pay only after the prospect keeps the qualified appointment and your team issues a documented estimate. Until payment tracking is fully connected, that is the cleanest result both sides can verify."
  • "What if your AI annoys my old customers?" → "Every message identifies your business by name and includes an easy way to opt out. Anyone who says stop is removed immediately and permanently."
  • "What if the estimate does not close?" → "The fee is for creating a verified estimate opportunity from a dormant contact. Your team's pricing, sales process, availability, and closing are outside 1APP's control, which is why the amount is a small fraction of the expected gross profit per estimate."
  • "Can this run away and cost me too much?" → "No. We can place a written campaign ceiling in the agreement and pause billable estimates when it is reached unless you approve an extension."
