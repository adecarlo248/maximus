### How to price the verified estimate

Use project-category data, not Riley's company-wide average and not its largest contract.

1. **Average collected job value:** average revenue actually collected from completed jobs in the selected project category.
2. **Gross margin:** `(collected revenue - direct labour/material/subcontractor costs) / collected revenue`.
3. **Estimate-to-sale close rate:** sold jobs divided by written estimates for that same category and a recent comparable period.
4. **Expected gross profit per estimate:** `average collected job value x gross margin x close rate`.
5. **Raw 1APP fee:** `expected gross profit per estimate x 20%`.
6. Round to the nearest $25, then use the lower of that result or the standard price-band ceiling.

Riley's stated figures produce: `$30,000 x 20% x 85% = $5,100` expected gross profit per estimate; `$5,100 x 20% = $1,020` raw fee; the $25,000–$49,999 band caps the fee at **$500**.

At a $30,000 average collected project and 20% margin, the close-rate sensitivity is:

| Verified project-estimate close rate | Expected gross profit per estimate | 20% fee calculation | Recommended fee after rounding/cap |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 20% | $1,200 | $240 | $250 |
| 30% | $1,800 | $360 | $350 |
| 40% | $2,400 | $480 | $475 |
| 50% | $3,000 | $600 | $500 cap |
| 85% stated | $5,100 | $1,020 | $500 cap |

At a 20% margin, Riley needs a category-specific close rate of approximately **41.7%** before the formula supports the full $500 band ceiling. If Percy cannot support the 85% claim with recent counts, use the credible category-specific rate or price provisionally at the lower supported amount.

The fee buys a **verified estimate opportunity**, not a closed job. A later win or loss does not change a properly earned fee because Riley controls its pricing, availability, sales process and close. A booking alone is never billable.
