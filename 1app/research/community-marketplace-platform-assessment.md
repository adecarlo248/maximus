# Community Marketplace Platform — 1APP Assessment and Partnership Proposal

**Prepared for:** Tony DeCarlo and Justin Roffey, 1APP Technologies Inc.  
**Source reviewed:** *Your Own Social Community Tech Stack* brochure, Community Marketplace Canada / Unified Community Communications  
**Status:** Strategic assessment only; vendor claims are unverified until due diligence is completed.

## Executive recommendation

Do **not** fold this platform into 1APP's standard service-business offer yet.

The platform appears to be a broad community ecosystem: private social network, business directory, marketplace, affiliate system, events, tickets, video, live conferencing, broadcasting, community TV, and media-production tools. That is materially different from 1APP's core promise of helping service businesses capture, follow up with, book, and convert leads.

The opportunity is a **separate 1APP community product line** aimed at organizations that already have a defined member base:

- Chambers of commerce and BIAs
- Sports associations and fundraising organizations
- Nonprofits and community foundations
- Municipal or neighbourhood initiatives
- Professional associations
- Franchise or dealer networks
- Large membership organizations

Proceed only through a **single paid pilot** after the vendor proves ownership rights, white-label rights, security, Canadian privacy compliance, API access, data portability, hosting reliability, support obligations, and commercial economics.

## 2. Fit with 1APP

### Strong strategic fit

The platform may fit when the buyer's primary goal is to **operate a member community and monetize participation**. 1APP can add the sales, onboarding, lead-management, follow-up, booking, and support layer around that community.

Potential 1APP value:

- Recruit businesses and members into the community
- Automate member and vendor onboarding
- Capture and route sponsor, vendor, and partnership enquiries
- Send approved email and SMS communications
- Schedule demos, onboarding sessions, events, and sponsor calls
- Track marketplace vendors and sponsors in pipelines
- Automate renewal, invoice, event, and follow-up reminders
- Provide AI voice and chat intake around the platform
- Build dashboards for partner acquisition, activation, and renewal

### Weak fit

It is not a natural core product for a plumber, roofer, contractor, or other single local service company. Those businesses primarily need lead response, booking, follow-up, estimates, reviews, customer communication, and reporting. Adding a social network, multi-vendor marketplace, community TV, and podcast studio creates complexity without solving their immediate revenue leak.

### Strategic risk

Selling this under the normal 1APP offer would blur 1APP's positioning. Buyers may stop understanding whether 1APP is a service-business automation company, a social network, a marketplace, a video platform, or a media-production company.

**Recommendation:** keep it as a distinct offer, such as **1APP Community Hub**, with its own ICP, demo, onboarding, pricing, support model, and sales materials.

### What 1APP already does well

- Lead and contact management
- Sales pipelines and opportunity tracking
- Online calendars and appointment booking
- Forms and website chat
- AI voice and conversation intake
- Missed-call response
- Email and SMS automation
- Appointment confirmations and reminders

- Community groups and internal activity feeds
- Community invitations and participation rewards
- Local business directory and offer marketplace
- Multi-vendor storefronts, affiliate commissions, and vendor payouts
- Community-wide event discovery and ticketing

## 4. Recommended operating model

### Positioning

**1APP Community Hub** helps membership organizations launch and operate a branded online community where members connect, local businesses participate, events are promoted, and sponsors gain measurable visibility—supported by 1APP onboarding, follow-up, automation, and reporting.

### Ideal customer profile

The best pilot organization should have:

- At least 250 reachable members or supporters
- A named executive sponsor
- At least one staff owner for community operations
- Existing local-business or sponsor relationships
- A clear reason members will return weekly
- Budget for launch, ongoing management, support, and content
- Willingness to participate in a 60-day pilot and case study

### Pilot success measures

- Invited members
- Account activations
- Weekly active members
- Businesses onboarded
- Offers or listings published
- Events created and registrations completed
- Sponsor inventory sold
- Marketplace transaction value, if enabled
- Member retention at Days 30 and 60
- Support volume and resolution time
- Revenue collected versus implementation and operating cost

## 5. Vendor due-diligence questions

### Corporate ownership and legal rights

1. What is the legal name and jurisdiction of the company that owns the software?
2. Who owns the source code and underlying intellectual property?
3. Do you have written authority to license, white-label, and permit resale of every included component?
4. What rights would 1APP receive: referral, reseller, agency, territory, white-label, or master-license rights?
5. Can 1APP use its own brand exclusively in front of customers?
6. Are any third-party logos, templates, plugins, media, or open-source components subject to separate terms?
7. What happens to 1APP clients if your business is sold, closes, or stops supporting the platform?

### Commercial terms

8. What are the one-time setup, monthly license, hosting, storage, bandwidth, support, update, and transaction costs?
9. Are fees charged per community, member, administrator, vendor, video minute, storage amount, message, or transaction?
10. What minimum commitments, contract terms, renewal rules, and price-increase rights apply?
11. Is there a sandbox or fully functional pilot environment?
12. What gross margin can 1APP retain as reseller or managed-service partner?
13. Who bills the end customer and who carries refund, chargeback, and tax responsibility?
14. Are marketplace or affiliate payouts handled by the platform, a processor, or the community operator?
15. Are there protected accounts, territories, or deal-registration rules?

### Security, privacy, and compliance

16. Where is customer data hosted and backed up?
17. Can Canadian client data remain in Canada?
18. Provide the privacy policy, data-processing agreement, subprocessors, retention policy, and breach-response process.
19. How does the platform support PIPEDA, CASL, consent records, deletion requests, and data export?
20. What encryption is used in transit and at rest?
21. Is multi-factor authentication available for administrators and members?
22. What role-based permissions, audit logs, moderation tools, and abuse-reporting tools exist?
23. What security testing, vulnerability management, penetration testing, or certifications are maintained?
24. How are payment information and marketplace payouts protected?
25. What accessibility standard does the interface meet?

### Technology and integration

26. Provide current API and webhook documentation.
27. Can contacts, consent, businesses, events, orders, transactions, subscriptions, and activity be synchronized with 1APP?
28. Which systems are the intended systems of record?
29. Does the platform support SSO, custom domains, DNS configuration, email authentication, and branded sender identities?
30. Are native iOS and Android apps included? If so, whose developer accounts and branding are used?
31. Which payment gateways, currencies, tax engines, shipping providers, and social platforms are supported today?
32. Are video hosting, live broadcasting, conferencing, and podcast tools native or third-party services?
33. What usage limits and overage rates apply to video, streaming, storage, bandwidth, and live events?
34. Can all data and media be exported in usable formats at any time?

### Reliability and support

35. Provide uptime history for the previous 12 months and the contractual SLA.
36. What backups, recovery objectives, disaster-recovery procedures, and status monitoring exist?
37. Who handles Level 1, Level 2, and engineering support?
38. What response and resolution targets apply by severity?
39. How are updates tested and communicated?
40. What implementation, training, documentation, and launch support is included?

### Proof and product maturity

41. Provide three current customer references using comparable functionality.
42. Provide live demonstrations of a production community, vendor marketplace, ticket purchase, affiliate payout, video upload, live broadcast, moderation case, and full data export.
43. What percentage of customers are still active after 12 months?
44. What are average member activation, weekly engagement, vendor activation, and transaction rates?
45. Which features in the brochure are live today, which are beta, and which require additional products or fees?

## 6. Red flags that stop the deal

Do not proceed if any of the following remains unresolved:

- Vendor cannot prove licensing and resale rights
- No usable data export
- No written privacy/security terms
- No Canadian privacy posture for Canadian communities
- Hidden transaction, storage, bandwidth, or video costs
- No API/webhook path for 1APP integration
- No production references
- No clear responsibility for marketplace payouts and chargebacks
- No uptime, backup, or support commitment
- Vendor requires 1APP to make promises the vendor will not contractually support

## 7. Draft partnership proposal

### Proposed title

**Pilot Partnership Proposal: 1APP Community Hub**

### Purpose

1APP Technologies Inc. proposes a controlled partnership to evaluate, configure, launch, and commercialize a branded community platform for Canadian membership organizations. The goal is to combine the vendor's community, marketplace, events, and media capabilities with 1APP's onboarding, automation, lead-management, communications, and customer-success systems.

### Pilot structure

**Phase 1 — Due diligence and technical validation (10 business days)**

- Execute mutual confidentiality terms
- Confirm ownership, licensing, reseller, and white-label rights
- Review security, privacy, hosting, data portability, and support documentation
- Validate APIs, webhooks, SSO, payments, marketplace payouts, and data export
- Agree on commercial terms and pilot responsibilities

**Phase 2 — Pilot design (10 business days)**

- Select one qualified community partner
- Define member, vendor, sponsor, and administrator journeys
- Choose pilot features; exclude unneeded features
- Establish the system of record for contacts, consent, payments, and reporting
- Configure brand, domain, roles, moderation, onboarding, and reporting
- Build 1APP automations for invitations, onboarding, reminders, sponsor enquiries, and support

**Phase 3 — Controlled launch (30 days)**

- Invite an initial member cohort
- Onboard a limited group of businesses and sponsors
- Launch a small number of events, offers, or listings
- Monitor activation, weekly use, support, moderation, and technical performance
- Hold weekly pilot reviews

**Phase 4 — Review and scale decision (Day 60)**

- Review commercial, technical, support, privacy, and engagement results
- Confirm required fixes and roadmap commitments
- Decide whether to stop, extend the pilot, or commercialize the offer
- No broad customer rollout without written approval from both parties

### Proposed responsibilities

**Vendor responsibilities**

- Platform license and lawful white-label/resale rights
- Hosting, updates, backups, security, and engineering support
- API/webhook documentation and technical assistance
- Data export and migration support
- Payment and marketplace technical documentation
- Administrator training and product documentation
- SLA and incident communication

**1APP responsibilities**

- Customer discovery and qualification
- Offer design and custom quoting
- Brand and onboarding configuration
- Lead, member, vendor, and sponsor follow-up automations
- Client training, adoption, and frontline support
- Pipeline, activation, and renewal reporting
- Sales and partner recruitment
- Pilot management and customer-success reviews

**Pilot customer responsibilities**

- Executive sponsor and community operator
- Member and business contact lists with lawful communication rights
- Brand assets, policies, content, and approvals
- Timely testing and feedback
- Community moderation and content ownership
- Participation in pilot measurement and final review

### Commercial framework for negotiation

1APP should request:

- Non-exclusive Canadian reseller and white-label rights during the pilot
- Protected deal registration for accounts introduced by 1APP
- A wholesale license that allows sufficient margin for sales, implementation, support, and customer success
- No undisclosed transaction or infrastructure fees
- Vendor-funded technical remediation for platform defects
- End-customer data ownership by the customer, with 1APP-authorized access
- Full export rights at all times
- A defined support escalation path and SLA
- Mutual permission required before using names, logos, or results in marketing

Recommended negotiation target: 1APP should retain enough gross margin to cover sales commissions, onboarding, support, and account management. Do not accept commercial terms until a cost model proves each deployment remains profitable under expected support and usage levels.

### Recommended offer to the pilot customer

Use a custom quote with two components:

1. **Launch and implementation fee** covering discovery, community design, branding, configuration, migration, automations, testing, administrator training, and launch management.
2. **Monthly managed-community subscription** covering the platform license, hosting allocation, 1APP automations, support, reporting, updates, and customer-success reviews.

Video, streaming, storage, marketplace transactions, payment fees, custom development, content production, and in-person services should be separately identified rather than hidden inside the base subscription.

### Decision request

Authorize a due-diligence meeting and product demonstration. No purchase, customer commitment, or public launch will occur until the vendor answers the due-diligence questions, provides a working sandbox, and both parties approve written pilot terms.

## 8. Suggested vendor meeting close

“The platform appears to add capabilities beyond 1APP's current community offering, particularly member networking, a local marketplace, event commerce, affiliate participation, and community media. We are interested in evaluating a Canadian reseller and managed-service partnership, but we need to validate ownership, security, privacy, APIs, data portability, full costs, and live customer results. If those checks pass, we propose one controlled 60-day pilot with agreed success measures before any broader rollout.”

## 9. Final decision

**Verdict: Conditional opportunity, not ready to buy.**

The platform could expand 1APP into community commerce and member engagement, but its breadth is also the risk. The brochure is feature-heavy and proof-light. The correct next step is a vendor diligence call and live sandbox review—not a commitment, rebrand, or customer sale.
