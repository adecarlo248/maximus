# 1APP Business and Revenue Audit

**Audit date:** October 1, 2026  
**Scope:** 1APP business files, current offer and pricing, Rod's training system, live website and conversion paths, connected CRM/calendar/conversations, Google Ads visibility, competitive positioning, and sales operating model.  
**Method:** Read-only inspection. No ads, website pages, workflows, contacts, or account settings were changed.

## Executive verdict

1APP does not primarily have an advertising problem. It has a disconnected revenue system.

The business has built a large product catalogue, website, blog library, training program, quote/handoff builder, CRM account, automations, and paid traffic before proving one narrow offer to one narrow buyer. The result is predictable: the ad, page, CTA, CRM record, sales conversation, delivery promise, and billing event do not tell one consistent story or produce one measurable funnel.

The fastest path to revenue is not more traffic, more content, more features, or more sales recruits. It is:

1. Select one beachhead market.
2. Sell one measurable entry offer.
3. Personally close and deliver three to five pilots.
4. Capture proof.
5. Install end-to-end attribution and a real pipeline.
6. Only then scale Rod, referrals, and paid search.

## Evidence found

### 1. CRM and pipeline

- 883 contacts are present in the connected 1APP account.
- 851 contact sources contain `CBRB`, indicating a large imported cold-prospect database rather than an active funnel.
- The contacts span unrelated categories and geographies, including trades, accountants, dental businesses, windows/doors, and other local businesses.
- The account contains **zero opportunities**.
- The single pipeline uses generic stages: New Lead, Contacted, Qualified, Proposal Sent, Negotiation, and Closed.
- There are no explicit stages for Diagnostic Booked, Diagnostic Held, Sprint Qualified, Sprint Live, Verified Estimate, Paid Expansion, Closed Won, Closed Lost, or Nurture.
- The stage probabilities are arbitrary and cannot support a trustworthy forecast.
- 28 conversations exist, including internal/test activity, inbound calls, spam, and some unread conversations.
- A small number of form and calendar-source contacts exist, but none became opportunities.
- The main demo calendar returned zero current events for August 1 through October 1, 2026.

**Diagnosis:** 1APP has a contact database, not a sales pipeline. There is no reliable owner, next action, stage conversion, forecast, or source-to-sale reporting.

### 2. Google Ads and attribution

- The 1APP Google Ads integration is not available in connected reporting.
- The reporting endpoint returned: `Cannot find google adwords id`.
- The Ad Manager endpoint returned no integration and no performance totals.
- No contacts were found with a Google source in the connected CRM.
- UTM/campaign/source data is not producing a usable click-to-contact-to-opportunity-to-client chain.
- The website contains Google tag `AW-18283853824`, but the commercial funnel cannot currently prove which spend produced a valid business lead, held diagnostic, proposal, Sprint, or paid client.
- Earlier website work used a shared conversion action/label across different conversion paths, which makes optimization less useful unless each business outcome is separated.

**Diagnosis:** Google may be buying clicks, but 1APP cannot currently audit or optimize the business result. Increasing budget would amplify uncertainty.

### 3. Website and conversion path

- The homepage promises broad "AI Automation for Small Businesses" and lists more than a dozen capabilities.
- It simultaneously promotes custom automation, a low entry price, a free quote/demo, a broad catalogue, and the Revenue Reactivation Sprint.
- The homepage says there are no locked-in tiers, but the FAQ still refers to Standard, Growth, and Revenue Engine tiers.
- The website promotes "starting as low as $99/month," which attracts self-serve/low-budget software shoppers while 1APP is delivering a managed implementation service.
- The Sprint page still describes percentage-based fees on attributed sales/collected revenue or gross profit.
- The current quote builder and latest Sprint playbook instead use a fixed fee per verified estimate.
- The homepage and sales files contain multiple versions of the trial/Sprint promise: 30-day trial, 14-day starter trial, free demo, free audit, performance fee, and fixed verified-estimate fee.
- The homepage does not present real customer case studies, quantified results, testimonial videos, recognizable client proof, or a before/after result from a completed Sprint.
- `/free-trial/`, `/get-started/`, and `/book-demo/` are not in the public sitemap.
- Key conversion pages lack canonical tags and useful structured data.
- The public Privacy Policy and Terms state that they are practical templates that should be reviewed by a lawyer. That warning should not appear on a live trust page.
- The main calendar description is generic, and the booking configuration asks for more appointment-title data than is needed.

**Diagnosis:** A prospect must understand too many offers and trust a company with no visible proof. The page explains features before establishing a credible, specific result.

### 4. Offer and pricing consistency

- The newest Sprint logic is: fixed cost per verified estimate until estimate-to-payment attribution is reliable.
- Older training modules, quote-governance files, website copy, and hiring documents still teach percentage success fees or a generic free trial.
- Current fixed verified-estimate bands may be a sensible starting hypothesis, but they have not been validated by completed client campaigns.
- The quote/technical handoff builder is thorough, but it collects a large amount of operational detail during the sales call. That can create friction before the buyer has committed.

**Diagnosis:** Rod can follow the official material and still give a prospect the wrong offer. Commercial terms must have one source of truth.

### 5. Sales-manager structure

- Rod's pack assigns a $5,000 monthly base, personal commissions, a direct-recruit override, a 20-client first-term target, and a target of 20 active certified sales associates.
- 1APP has not yet validated one repeatable offer, funnel, close rate, delivery time, or expansion rate.
- The training system is comprehensive but does not enforce a simple activity model tied to a named-account list, live conversations, held diagnostics, proposals, pilots, and expansions.
- The title and recruiting target encourage management before there is a repeatable sales motion to manage.

**Diagnosis:** 1APP is trying to scale a sales organization before proving founder-led sales. Rod should operate as the founding salesperson/business developer until the offer converts predictably.

### 6. Lead response and messaging

- The missed-call reply is generic, contains awkward spacing, gives two next steps, and drops a raw calendar link.
- A form lead received obsolete "30 day free trial" language.
- Some conversations are unread and no opportunity is created automatically.
- Some contacts contain SMS delivery/DND errors.

**Diagnosis:** Even the limited inbound activity is not entering a disciplined five-minute response and opportunity-management process.

## Competitive benchmark

Successful competitors do not win merely because they have more features.

### Housecall Pro

- Leads with one category: software to run and grow a home-service business.
- Shows strong proof immediately: 200,000+ pros, 100M+ jobs, reported average revenue growth, time saved, reviews, and customer stories.
- Uses industry- and team-size-based packaging.
- Offers a 14-day trial with no card and visible entry pricing.
- Source: https://www.housecallpro.com/ and https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/

### Podium

- Leads with one result: convert local-business conversations into revenue with a 24/7 AI employee.
- States recognizable outcomes: faster response, booked appointments, dormant database revenue, and less staff workload.
- Builds separate industry pages and displays a large installed-business trust signal.
- Uses demo-led sales instead of trying to explain every technical component on one page.
- Source: https://www.podium.com/ and https://www.podium.com/solutions/home-services/

### Smith.ai

- Defines exactly what is included, what usage costs, what add-ons cost, and how calls are handled.
- Uses transparent plan thresholds, dedicated numbers, integrations, qualification, booking, and real case studies.
- Provides a clear consultation path and a money-back guarantee.
- Source: https://smith.ai/pricing

### NiceJob

- Owns a narrow category: reputation marketing.
- Uses simple plans, a 14-day trial, no contract, support access, and clear add-on costs.
- Connects features to outcomes such as more reviews, repeat campaigns, and bookings.
- Source: https://get.nicejob.com/pricing

### What 1APP should copy

- One buyer and one urgent result per landing page.
- Immediate proof above the fold.
- A real demo of the exact workflow.
- Clear commercial boundaries and usage charges.
- Industry-specific pages and examples.
- Case studies that state baseline, implementation, result, and time period.
- A single CTA and a short qualification step.

### What 1APP should not copy

- An all-in-one feature war.
- Low self-serve software pricing while providing high-touch custom service.
- Unverified percentage or ROI claims.
- Free custom implementation before scope, list quality, and economics are qualified.

## Recommended market and entry offer

### Beachhead market

For the next 90 days, focus on one group:

**Ontario home-improvement contractors with average jobs of roughly $5,000-$25,000 and at least 250 dormant estimates, past leads, or past customers.**

Best first sub-niches:

- Waterproofing and foundation repair
- Roofing and exterior renovation
- Windows and doors
- HVAC replacement/installation
- Deck, fence, and renovation contractors

Avoid broad outreach to medical, dental, accounting, and unrelated industries until the offer and compliance process are proven.

### Entry offer

Use one front-end offer:

**1APP 14-Day Dormant Estimate Reactivation Sprint**

- Re-engage a qualified, client-authorized list.
- Use one approved offer and one appointment/estimate path.
- No upfront implementation fee for a tightly templated pilot.
- Client pays disclosed messaging usage or receives a small defined allowance.
- 1APP is paid a fixed fee per verified estimate, subject to a written campaign ceiling.
- A verified estimate requires a kept qualified appointment and a written estimate with evidence.
- Percentage revenue share stays off until estimate-to-payment tracking is proven.
- The Sprint starts only after data, number, calendar, messages, compliance attestation, and testing are complete.

The current fee bands are starting hypotheses, not permanent prices. Recalibrate after ten verified estimates in a niche.

## Website repair

Do not send paid traffic to the generic homepage.

Build one landing page for the chosen niche and Sprint:

1. Headline: `Turn dormant [roofing/waterproofing] estimates into new sales conversations in 14 days.`
2. Subhead: client-authorized past leads; fixed fee per verified estimate; campaign ceiling.
3. Three-step process: qualify list, launch campaign, verify estimates.
4. Eligibility: niche, geography, database size, average job, consent basis, calendar capacity.
5. Exact definition of a verified estimate.
6. A realistic worked example with no revenue guarantee.
7. Proof section with real pilot data.
8. Founder/team identity and local contact information.
9. One CTA: `Check if your dormant list qualifies.`
10. Short form: name, business, phone, email, niche, approximate dormant contacts, average job value.

Remove from that paid page:

- The 12-feature catalogue
- `Starting at $99/month`
- Generic free-demo language
- Conflicting trial and percentage-fee language
- Multiple CTAs

Keep the broad homepage for branded visitors, but make it consistent with the single current offer and paid expansion path.

## CRM and sales operating system

### Required pipeline

1. Named Account
2. Contacted
3. Live Conversation
4. Diagnostic Booked
5. Diagnostic Held
6. Sprint Qualified
7. Proposal Sent
8. Accepted / Onboarding
9. Sprint Live
10. Results Review
11. Paid Expansion
12. Closed Won

Use separate terminal stages: Closed Lost, Not Qualified, and Nurture.

Every open opportunity must have:

- One owner
- One next action
- One due date
- Source and campaign
- Estimated value
- Written stage-exit evidence
- Loss/no-decision reason when closed

### Initial activity model

Until real conversion data exists, use this weekly operating target:

- 100 named ICP accounts added/researched
- 100 personalized outbound attempts
- 10 live owner conversations
- 4 held diagnostics
- 2 Sprint-qualified proposals
- 1 pilot launch

These are operating hypotheses, not guaranteed benchmarks. Review actual conversion every Friday and adjust after four weeks.

### Inbound service-level agreement

- Respond to calls/forms during business hours in under five minutes.
- One missed-call text, one CTA, and an immediate owner task.
- Call twice during the first business day if the lead is qualified and reachable.
- Create the opportunity automatically.
- Stop automated sales follow-up when a person replies and assign a human.

## Google Ads repair

### Immediate

- Do not increase the budget.
- Pause or sharply cap broad generic campaigns until data is exported and the funnel is repaired.
- Obtain the last 90 days of campaign, search-term, keyword, ad, device, geography, cost, click, and conversion data.
- Separate real business leads from tests, spam, and button-click conversions.

### Restart conditions

Restart one controlled Search campaign only after:

- One niche landing page is live.
- Test click -> landing page -> form/call -> contact -> opportunity -> held diagnostic -> outcome is verified.
- Separate conversion actions exist for valid lead, booked diagnostic, held diagnostic, Sprint accepted, and paid client.
- At least three pilots have run and at least one real proof asset exists.
- Search terms use exact/phrase intent; broad match, Display, and Search Partners remain off until proven.
- A written stop-loss rule exists based on acceptable CAC and qualified diagnostics, not raw leads.

The Sprint is likely better sold first through targeted outbound, warm introductions, and partners than through broad search. Buyers rarely search for "dormant estimate reactivation Sprint" by name.
