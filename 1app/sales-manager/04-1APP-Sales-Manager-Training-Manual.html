1APP Sales Manager Training Manual

Version: 1.1 Draft Owner: 1APP Technologies Inc. Audience: Sales Manager and approved sales associates Brand rule: In client-facing communication, refer only to 1APP and approved 1APP feature names.

1. What the Sales Manager is responsible for

The Sales Manager is not just the best closer. The Sales Manager builds a sales system that other people can follow without creating bad deals for fulfillment.

The role owns:

  • Recruiting suitable associates.
  • Training and certifying them before independent selling.
  • Daily pipeline discipline and follow-up.
  • Call coaching and role-play.
  • Accurate forecasting.
  • Offer, pricing, conduct, and brand controls.
  • Complete sales-to-fulfillment handoffs.
  • Trial conversion planning.
  • Team output and quality.

The Sales Manager does not control:

  • Unapproved discounts or custom pricing exceptions.
  • Technical feasibility.
  • Launch approval.
  • Custom integrations or development promises.
  • Contract changes.
  • Refunds, credits, or waived fees.

2. What 1APP sells

The plain-English description

1APP is a managed automation system for service businesses. It helps capture leads, respond faster, book appointments, follow up consistently, organize opportunities, request reviews, and reduce repetitive front-office work.

1APP is not sold as generic software access. The value is diagnosis, configuration, testing, training, and support around the client's real operating process.

Current public offer

  • Custom automation starting as low as $99/month.
  • 14-day free trial.
  • No credit card required to begin the advertised trial.
  • Final price is quote-based and depends on the selected automations, setup, usage, integrations, and support.

What the starting price does not mean

Never say:

  • “Everything is $99.”
  • “Every business gets the same package.”
  • “There is no setup fee” unless the approved quote says so.
  • “The trial includes every feature.”
  • “It will guarantee leads, sales, rankings, or savings.”

The sales principle

Sell the smallest useful automation set that solves the prospect's highest-priority measurable problem. Do not feature-dump.

3. Ideal customer profile

Best-fit businesses

  • Established local service or trades business.
  • Receives calls, forms, messages, quote requests, bookings, or repeat-service opportunities.
  • Loses opportunities through missed calls, slow response, inconsistent follow-up, no-shows, forgotten estimates, weak review collection, or scattered records.
  • Has a decision-maker who will participate.
  • Will provide the information and access needed for setup.
  • Can evaluate success during a 14-day trial and pay after conversion.

Strong initial verticals

  • Waterproofing and foundation repair.
  • Roofing.
  • HVAC.
  • Plumbing.
  • Electrical.
  • Landscaping and lawn care.
  • Cleaning.
  • Painting.
  • Construction and renovation.
  • Other appointment-, quote-, or follow-up-driven local services.

Poor fit or disqualify

  • Prospect demands guaranteed leads or revenue.
  • No real operating business or customers.

  • Required outcome is outside approved capabilities.

4. Automation catalogue and what each item solves

Missed-call text-back

Problem: The business misses a call and the prospect contacts the next company. Outcome: The caller receives an immediate approved text and can reply or book. Setup requires: Business number/route, approved message, hours, owner alerts, opt-out behavior, escalation rules, and testing.

AI voice agent

Problem: Calls go unanswered or staff repeatedly answer the same questions. Outcome: Approved calls can be answered, qualified, routed, or booked. Setup requires: Purpose, greeting, knowledge, services, service area, hours, questions, booking rules, transfer rules, emergency rules, prohibited claims, fallback, and testing.

Appointment booking and reminders

Problem: Back-and-forth scheduling and no-shows. Outcome: Prospects can book approved times and receive confirmations/reminders. Setup requires: Calendar owner, timezone, appointment types, availability, duration, buffers, locations, assignment, confirmation/reminder copy, reschedule/cancel rules, and test bookings.

CRM and opportunity pipeline

Problem: Leads are scattered across phones, inboxes, notes, or memory. Outcome: Each lead has an owner, stage, next action, and history. Setup requires: Stage definitions, owners, lead sources, required fields, duplication rules, notifications, reporting, and user permissions.

New-lead follow-up

Problem: Leads wait too long or never receive consistent follow-up. Outcome: Approved SMS/email/tasks begin after an inquiry and stop when the person replies, books, opts out, or is disqualified. Setup requires: Trigger, channels, consent basis, copy, timing, business-hour rules, stop conditions, owner, and alerts.

Estimate or quote follow-up

Problem: Quotes are sent but not followed up. Outcome: The prospect receives timely approved reminders and the sales team sees who needs human follow-up. Setup requires: Quote source, sent/accepted/paid signals, estimate link, payment/deposit process, timing, stop conditions, owner tasks, and testing.

Review requests

Problem: Happy customers are not consistently asked to leave a review. Outcome: Eligible completed jobs trigger a compliant review request. Setup requires: Completion trigger, review link, timing, exclusions, complaint handling, and stop conditions.

Reactivation and nurture

Problem: Old leads and past customers are ignored. Outcome: Approved segments receive timely re-engagement messages. Setup requires: Clean data, consent/opt-out status, segment, offer, timing, reply owner, and suppression rules.

Invoice/payment reminders

Problem: Staff manually chase outstanding balances. Outcome: Approved reminders and secure payment paths are sent based on billing status. Setup requires: Billing connection, authorization, status triggers, secure link, timing, exceptions, escalation, and test transaction where practical.

Website/form/chat capture

Problem: Website inquiries do not reliably enter the sales process. Outcome: Leads are captured, attributed, assigned, and followed up. Setup requires: Website access, approved form fields, destination, tracking, consent language, notifications, and end-to-end testing.

Reporting and analytics

Problem: The owner cannot see lead response, bookings, follow-up, or pipeline. Outcome: Agreed metrics are visible and reviewed. Setup requires: Metric definitions, sources, owners, reporting cadence, and data-quality checks.

5. Sales process

Stage 1 — New Lead

Required before advancement:

  • Deduplicated contact and business.
  • Lead source, owner, niche, and location.
  • First task and due date.
  • Response attempted or sent.

Stage 2 — Contacted

Required before advancement:

  • Two-way contact.
  • Basic fit confirmed.
  • Business problem identified.
  • Decision-maker identified or accessible.
  • Audit/demo booked or clear nurture/loss reason.

Stage 3 — Free Quote/Demo Booked

Required:

  • Date/time confirmed.
  • Decision-maker invited.
  • Website and business researched.
  • Likely leak and relevant workflow prepared.

Stage 4 — Automation Audit Completed

Required:

  • Current process documented.
  • Pain and quantified impact recorded.
  • Desired outcome and urgency confirmed.
  • Decision process and objections recorded.
  • Recommended automation set identified.
  • Dated next step agreed.

Stage 5 — Custom Quote Sent

Required:

  • Named automation line items.
  • Scope and exclusions.
  • Setup requirements and client dependencies.
  • Total post-trial monthly price.
  • Setup fee, taxes, usage assumptions, and support.
  • Trial scope, trigger, dates, and success measures.
  • Follow-up task.

Stage 6 — Quote Accepted — Trial Setup

Required:

  • Written acceptance.
  • Complete handoff.
  • Onboarding form sent.
  • Fulfillment acceptance before work begins.

Stage 7 — Trial Live

Required:

  • Automation is live and usable.
  • Official trial start/end dates recorded.
  • Client knows week-one actions and support path.
  • Success measures are being captured.

Stage 8 — Trial Review / Conversion

Required:

  • Value evidence reviewed.
  • Concerns addressed.
  • Continue, modify, or end decision scheduled.

Stage 9 — Won — Paid Active

Required:

  • Written paid-continuation authorization.
  • Billing authorization.
  • First required payment cleared.
  • Client and commission records updated.

Stage 10 — Lost / Trial Ended

Required:

  • Structured loss reason.
  • Offboarding where applicable.
  • Future follow-up date when appropriate.

6. Discovery framework

Use SPIN: Situation, Problem, Implication, Need-payoff.

Opening

We have about 20 minutes. I want to understand how leads, bookings, estimates, and follow-up work today, identify where opportunities or time are being lost, and then show only the 1APP automations that match those problems. If there is no fit, we can say that directly. Fair?

Situation

  • How do new leads reach you today?
  • Who answers calls, forms, texts, and social messages?
  • What tools do you use for calendars, contacts, estimates, payments, and reviews?

Do not spend the whole meeting collecting basic facts that could have been researched.

Problem

  • What happens when a call is missed?
  • How quickly are web leads contacted?
  • Where do bookings, quotes, reminders, or reviews fall through?
  • Which repetitive task consumes the most staff or owner time?

Implication

  • How many opportunities are affected in a typical week?
  • What is an average completed job worth?
  • What happens when follow-up is one day late?
  • How much owner/admin time does this consume?
  • How does this affect customer experience or staff workload?

Need-payoff

  • If every missed caller received an immediate response, what would improve?
  • If quote follow-up happened consistently, what would a few additional booked jobs mean?
  • What would success need to look like by Day 14 for you to continue?

Qualification standard

Do not advance unless there is:

  • A real business.
  • A measurable problem.
  • Decision access.
  • A deliverable 1APP use case.
  • Willingness to approve a custom quote and trial scope.
  • A reason to act within 90 days.

7. Demo framework

Rule

Show the problem being solved, not the entire application.

Recommended flow

  1. Restate the prospect's exact problem.
  2. Show the triggering event.
  3. Show what 1APP does automatically.
  4. Show the human alert or task.
  5. Show where the contact/opportunity is recorded.
  6. Show the result the client will measure during the trial.
  7. Confirm the workflow matches their process.

Demo close

Based on what you showed me, I recommend starting with [automation set] because it directly addresses [problem and impact]. I’ll prepare the custom quote with the exact trial scope, setup requirements, post-trial monthly amount, and success measures. If that matches what we discussed, is there anything else that would prevent you from starting onboarding?

8. Quote and trial rules

Every quote must include:

  • Client problem and desired outcome.
  • Each automation by name.
  • Trigger, actions, channels, and integrations.
  • Required access and client responsibilities.
  • Included usage or assumptions.
  • Trial scope and success measure.
  • Trial start trigger and end date.
  • Post-trial monthly price.
  • Setup fee, taxes, and billing cadence.
  • Explicit exclusions.

Trial clock begins when the quoted automation is live and usable—not when the quote is accepted.

No paid-active win or recurring commission exists until the client completes the trial, continues, authorizes billing, and the first recurring payment clears. A one-time personal setup-fee commission may be earned earlier only after the approved setup payment clears under Schedule A; that payment does not make the client paid-active or count toward Rod's target.

10. Sales-to-fulfillment handoff

The handoff is a quality gate, not paperwork.

Before submission, the Sales Manager must be able to answer:

  • Who is the client and decision-maker?
  • What exact problem are we solving?
  • What did we quote?
  • What is included and excluded?
  • What starts and stops each automation?
  • What access and information are required?
  • What can the AI say and never say?
  • What is an emergency and who receives it?
  • What is the trial success measure?
  • What launch date was discussed and why?
  • What promise did sales make?

Fulfillment may accept, return, or require scope review. No setup starts until accepted.

11. Trial conversion process

  • Day 1: Confirm automation live and trial clock started.
  • Day 3: Confirm first value event or unblock activation.
  • Day 7: Adoption and issue review.
  • Day 10: Surface conversion risk, gather value evidence, and restate the approved post-trial quote.
  • Day 12: Schedule the conversion decision and confirm any approved scope changes.
  • Day 14: Convert to paid, approve a revised quote, or offboard.

Conversion conversation

We agreed success would look like [measure]. During the trial, 1APP produced [verified evidence] and we addressed [issues]. The approved ongoing service is [scope] at [price] plus applicable tax. Would you like to continue with the approved scope, adjust it, or end the trial?

Never claim causation the data does not support.

12. Objection handling

Use: Acknowledge → Clarify → Reframe → Evidence → Advance.

“It’s too expensive.”

Is the concern the monthly amount itself, or whether the automation will recover enough time or opportunity to justify it? Let’s compare the quote with the specific missed leads or admin cost you described.

“I need to think about it.”

Of course. What specific part needs thought—scope, price, timing, trust, or who else must approve? If we identify that now, I can give you the right information instead of chasing you with generic follow-up.

“We already have software.”

That may be fine. The question is whether the current setup reliably produces the response, booking, follow-up, and visibility you need. What is working well, and where does the process still break?

“Will it bring me more leads?”

1APP does not guarantee lead volume. It helps the business respond, book, follow up, and measure more consistently so fewer existing opportunities are lost. If lead generation is included, it must be separately scoped and measured.

“Can you customize anything?”

We can configure approved automations around your workflow. I won’t promise a custom feature or integration until fulfillment reviews feasibility, effort, support impact, and price.

“Why do you need all this onboarding information?”

The automation acts on your customers and your calendar. Hours, services, booking, escalation, and stop rules prevent wrong messages and bad bookings. Clean setup is what makes the system safe and useful.

13. Recruiting and training the sales team

Who to recruit

  • Comfortable speaking with business owners.
  • Coachable and consistent.
  • Willing to use 1APP as the system of record.
  • Has reliable access to a laptop or desktop computer, stable internet, a webcam or headset, and a multi-factor-authentication method.
  • Has general computer skills: browser tabs, email, calendars, video calls, forms, file uploads, basic spreadsheets, password managers, and routine troubleshooting.
  • Has practical AI literacy: can write a clear prompt, review output for accuracy, correct unsupported claims, and follow approved data-handling rules.
  • Willing and able to guide a new client through the approved onboarding checklist by screen share.
  • Understands service-business pain.
  • Can follow approved claims and pricing controls.
  • Values long-term client fit over quick commission.

Do not recruit

  • People seeking passive money with no activity.
  • People who exaggerate results or income.
  • People who refuse CRM accountability.
  • People who discount or improvise offers.
  • People who mishandle client information.
  • People who want to build an unapproved downline.
  • People without reliable access to an approved computer and internet connection.
  • People who fail the computer, 1APP, or responsible-AI practical assessment.
  • People who paste client passwords, payment information, personal data, or confidential business information into unapproved AI tools.

Recruiting conversation

1APP helps service businesses automate lead response, booking, follow-up, reviews, and repetitive admin. The sales role is to identify good-fit businesses, run disciplined discovery, use approved offers, and create clean handoffs. Compensation is tied to collected client revenue; there are no guaranteed earnings. Before selling independently, every associate must complete training and pass certification.

Associate onboarding sequence

  1. Interview and fit scorecard.
  2. Approved agreement signed.
  3. Identity and administrative setup.
  4. Device, browser, email, video-call, password-manager, and multi-factor-authentication check.
  5. Company, buyer, offer, product, sales, 1APP, responsible-AI, conduct, privacy, onboarding, and handoff training.
  6. Knowledge tests.
  7. Computer, 1APP, AI, pitch, discovery, demo, objection, onboarding, and handoff practicals.
  8. Certification decision.
  9. Restricted launch with first three calls, opportunities, and client onboarding sessions reviewed.
  10. Weekly coaching and monthly standing review.

What a certified associate may do during onboarding

  • Guide the client through the approved onboarding checklist.
  • Confirm business identity, services, hours, service area, booking rules, escalation contacts, and approved messaging.
  • Help the client locate approved information and upload non-secret assets through the approved process.
  • Record requirements, questions, dependencies, and promised next steps in 1APP.
  • Demonstrate the approved client experience using test data.
  • Escalate technical, privacy, security, billing, or scope questions to the designated owner.

What an associate may not do

  • Ask a client to send passwords, recovery codes, payment-card data, or private API keys through chat, email, notes, or forms.
  • Change live automations, phone settings, integrations, payment settings, user permissions, domains, or billing.
  • Import or export client data without written approval and separate training.
  • Publish, launch, or approve an automation.
  • Make technical promises or change scope, pricing, terms, or timelines.
  • Access any client account unless a named owner approved the exact client, task, permissions, and expiry time.

14. Manager coaching standard

Weekly one-to-one, 30 minutes

  • 10 minutes: pipeline and top three deals.
  • 10 minutes: one skill using a real call or message.
  • 10 minutes: associate commitment and manager support.

Call review dimensions

  • Opening and agenda.
  • Discovery depth.
  • Listening.
  • Value tied to pain.
  • Objection handling.
  • Clear next step with date.
  • Accuracy and approved claims.
  • CRM notes and handoff quality.

Coaching rule

Coach one specific behavior at a time. “Close more” is not coaching. “Quantify the cost of missed calls before showing the demo” is coaching.
