Google Ads Recovery Plan

A plain-English call script, evidence summary, recovery roadmap, and information checklist for rebuilding Google Ads around qualified leads, booked estimates, and won jobs.

Core message: Google is still sending traffic. The system is failing to reliably identify which clicks become real opportunities—and some of the traffic is poorly matched to waterproofing services. The fix is measurement first, then a controlled campaign rebuild.
Prepared by 1APP Technologies Inc. • For discussion with Peterborough Waterproofing
Page 1 • Your call plan

Lead the conversation—do not defend the old ads

Opening script — read this

“You were right to flag that the ads are not producing the same business response. I reviewed the traffic, tracking, website handoff, and 1APP pipeline. Google is still generating clicks, but too much of the traffic is either the wrong intent or is not being tracked through to an estimate and completed job. I do not recommend increasing the budget. I recommend fixing measurement first, rebuilding around high-intent waterproofing searches, and connecting every lead to immediate follow-up. Then we optimize around booked estimates and won jobs—not clicks.”

Start with their experience

  • “When did you first notice the change?”
  • “What changed—fewer calls, fewer forms, worse leads, fewer estimates, or fewer jobs?”
  • “Which services used to perform best?”
  • “Was anything changed in the account, website, phone handling, budget, or service area around that time?”
Do not say: “The ads are broken” or “AI will fix it.” Tracking is incomplete, so the data cannot prove the exact number of real leads. Say the current system cannot reliably connect ad spend to business outcomes.

Phase 1 — Stop waste and establish the baseline
Outcome: clean starting point

Keep high-volume paused campaigns paused. Do not raise the active budget. Review Change History, Search Terms, location, device, time, network, and conversion settings. Confirm the exact date and nature of the performance decline.

Phase 2 — Repair tracking and attribution
Outcome: every lead has a source

Connect Google Ads to 1APP Ad Manager, add tracking parameters, capture click IDs and campaign data, repair forms, and test calls/forms from click through opportunity creation and conversion recording.

Phase 3 — Rebuild high-intent Search campaigns
Outcome: relevant traffic

Separate basement waterproofing, foundation/crack repair, mould removal/testing, and—if approved—sump pump/drainage searches. Start with Phrase and Exact match. Keep Display and Search Partners off until Search is proven.

Phase 4 — Match each ad to the right page
Outcome: more qualified inquiries

Use a focused page for each service with service/geography headline, mobile click-to-call, short estimate form, warranty/proof, relevant photos, and a confirmation event that records the conversion.

Phase 5 — Activate 1APP lead response
Outcome: faster follow-up

Create one clean opportunity, notify Justin and Belinda, text the lead immediately, assign a five-minute call task, and follow up at 15 minutes, two hours, Day 1, Day 3, and Day 7 until reply, booking, opt-out, or disqualification.

Phase 6 — Feed quality and revenue back
Outcome: optimize for jobs

Record Qualified Lead, Estimate Booked, and Job Won—with job value where possible. Review weekly cost per qualified lead, booked estimate, won job, and return on ad spend.

Page 4 • 1APP implementation

What 1APP will do

Tracking and lead capture

  • Connect Google Ads reporting and Ad Manager.
  • Capture source, campaign, ad group, keyword, match type, landing page, and click ID.
  • Repair/test website forms, thank-you events, and tracked calls.
  • Create or update one contact and one sales opportunity per real inquiry.

Response automation

  • Immediate customer acknowledgement.
  • Justin and Belinda alerts.
  • Five-minute call task during business hours.
  • Timed follow-up until reply, booking, opt-out, or disqualification.
  • Appointment confirmations and reminders.

Campaign and page rebuild

  • Separate campaigns/ad groups by approved service.
  • Build high-intent keyword and negative-keyword lists.
  • Draft service-specific ads and extensions.
  • Build or repair focused landing pages.
  • Publish only after client review and written approval.

Measurement and optimization

  • Track qualified leads, estimates, jobs, and revenue.
  • Send approved offline conversions back to Google.
  • Build spend-without-results and slow-response alerts.
  • Provide a weekly scorecard by service/campaign.
Success is not “more clicks.” Success means the business can see how many paid-search leads became qualified, how many booked estimates, how many became jobs, what those jobs were worth, and what each result cost.

Scope boundaries to state clearly

  • Google ad spend is separate and paid through their Google Ads account.
  • Campaign setup, landing pages, tracking, 1APP workflows, and ongoing management are implementation work to be quoted.
  • No one can guarantee lead volume or sales. 1APP can improve control, measurement, speed, and decision quality.
  • No rebuilt campaign goes live without approved budget, targeting, copy, pages, and written launch approval.
Page 5 • Discovery checklist

Information you need from Peterborough Waterproofing

1. When did performance begin to decline, and what changed?
2. What does “not responding the same” mean: fewer calls, fewer forms, lower-quality leads, fewer estimates, lower close rate, or lower job value?
3. Which services should receive budget during the next 60 days, in priority order?
4. Which towns/postal-code areas are priority, acceptable, and excluded?
5. Average completed-job value by service?
6. Approximate gross-margin range by service?
7. Of every 10 completed estimates, approximately how many become jobs?
8. Monthly ad budget and maximum acceptable cost per booked estimate or won job?
9. Weekly capacity: how many new estimates and jobs can the team handle without hurting service?
10. Who owns new-lead response? Who is the backup? What are the approved business hours?
11. Which calls count as urgent (for example, active water), and how should they be escalated?
Page 6 • Access and proof

Access, assets, and approvals required

Account and technical access

  • Google Ads administrative access.
  • Google Analytics / Tag Manager access, if used.
  • Website/domain access for tags, forms, pages, and confirmation events.
  • Approved phone numbers, forwarding behaviour, and call-routing rules.
  • Access/approval to connect Google Ads with 1APP Ad Manager.
  • Permission to test one real call and one real form path.

Historical evidence

  • Best-performing date range before decline.
  • Previous agency/manager changes, if any.
  • Known changes to budgets, targeting, website, phone number, forms, or service area.
  • List of leads/jobs believed to have come from Google during the audit window.

Sales and proof assets

  • Approved warranty language, including the 25-year transferable warranty.
  • Best reviews and permission to use them.
  • Before/after project photos by service.
  • Approved service descriptions and exclusions.
  • Free-estimate terms and any qualifying requirements.
  • Preferred offers and claims the business will stand behind.

Written approvals

  • Priority services and geography.
  • Daily/monthly budget and stop-loss guardrail.
  • Campaign structure, keywords, negatives, copy, and pages.
  • 1APP customer messages and lead-routing rules.
  • Launch approval after end-to-end tracking tests pass.
Credential rule: use supported account invitations and OAuth connections. Do not ask them to text or email passwords, recovery codes, private keys, or hidden tokens.

Page 7 • Objections and close

Answers to likely questions

They may askYour answer
“Why did it work before?”“That is what Change History and the historical comparison will tell us. Competition, search behaviour, targeting, website changes, and tracking can all affect results. I do not want to guess; I want to identify the exact change.”
“Can AI fix the ads?”“AI can speed up research, drafts, analysis, lead response, and reporting. It does not replace strategy or approval. We use it inside a controlled system with human review.”
“Should we spend more?”“Not yet. More budget magnifies whatever already exists. First we prove tracking and traffic quality; then we increase spend only when qualified-lead and job economics support it.”
“Do we need a new website?”“Not necessarily. We likely need focused service landing pages and reliable conversion events. We can keep the main site and repair the paid-traffic path.”
“How fast will results improve?”“Tracking should be testable before launch. Campaign performance needs real search and conversion data. We will review early signals weekly, but I will not promise a result before the system collects clean data.”
“What will this cost?”“Google ad spend stays separate. After today’s discovery and access review, 1APP will provide a clear quote for tracking, campaign rebuild, landing pages, automation, and any ongoing management.”
